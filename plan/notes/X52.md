# X52 — the circle tangent to two lines is a four-click figure

## What was measured

- The circle cell declared a fourth method, `sketch.create.circle.tangent`, as `icon: None, run:
  None` (`crates/ui/src/toolbars/spec.rs`), so the list drew a blank row that ran nothing — the
  owner's report, and the whole of the entry.
- There is no channel for picking an existing line: `app/src/gizmo_math.rs` snaps to draft ends, the
  10 mm grid, the u/v axis through the previous point and a surface point (`SnapTarget`), and a
  viewport click resolves to a document `Id` (`crates/ui/src/target.rs`), never to a segment's
  direction. "Two picked line targets" is therefore a new pick channel, not a drawing tool.
- A typed number reaches a draft only as the point it places: `PointState::placed` → `apply_click` →
  `DrawDraft::push`, and `path_with` builds the figure from the stored points alone (plan/notes/C4.md).
  The side count is the only value carried beside the points, through `DrawDraft::set_sides`.
- `crates/ui/src/toolbars.rs` `CONTOUR_TOOLS` is the shell's key path and the Settings shortcut list,
  and a test demands a key for every tool in it: a cell's second method gets no key (C4).
- `DrawDraft::preview` returned `TooFewPoints` for any fixed figure still short of its points, so the
  status bar warned "the drawing needs at least N points" for the whole of a three- or four-click
  gesture — visible on the arc tool too, and no test held it.
- `FieldParam::Radius` already exists in the point atom: nothing new was needed to name the number.
- A typed `0` releases a lock instead of locking the number (C4), so a frame test cannot place a
  point at `0` or name a `0` degree direction.

## Decisions

- Four clicks: the corner where the two lines meet, a point on each of them, then the centre. Two
  lines through a corner need the corner plus one direction each, which is three clicks; the fourth
  names the wedge and the radius at once — the pointer's side picks one of the four wedges, and its
  distance along that wedge's bisector is the radius, so a typed number and the hand agree.
- The fourth point is the centre itself, so the commit needs no second channel: `path_with` reads the
  radius back as the perpendicular distance from the placed centre to the first line, and the second
  line is the same distance away because the legs and the centre came from one wedge. Rounding is the
  micrometre every rounded figure in a path takes.
- The ring is a plain `Path::circle_through` of that centre and a rim one radius away, exactly as
  `apothem_rim` and `edge_rim` build a polygon's rim in the draft: no model change, no schema bump,
  material and name come from the same `object.path.create` the other circle methods use.
- Stage 2 and 3 are `Shape::Direction` — the click names an angle and the figure reads no distance —
  so the plates ask for the angle alone and the placed point keeps the pointer's reach.
- `DrawDraftError::NoWedge` refuses two legs on one line: the third click is stored, and the very
  next pointer frame reports it through `preview`, so the person sees the reason before the fourth
  click rather than after it.
- `preview` now answers `Ok(None)` for `TooFewPoints`: a figure that is still short of its points has
  nothing to show and nothing to refuse, and the status line stops naming a click nobody made.
- The two lines are drawn: `PointState` gained `legs`, painted with the same dashed construction guide
  the chord uses, the live one following the pointer until both are placed. Without them the first
  three clicks land in an empty viewport.
- The icon is the ring in `draw` at `1.5` with the two lines in `view` at `1.3` crossing at the lower
  left and running well past their tangency points, because the circle is what the person gets.

## Ruled out

- A segment-pick channel for "two picked lines": it would touch the snap table, the viewport pick and
  the editor's pointer state for one method, and the corner grammar reaches every crossing pair with
  clicks the draft already takes.
- A hidden radius slot beside `sides`: C4 closed that door, and a typed number is a promise for one
  point while `sides` is the figure's own count, not a point.
- Three clicks — corner, one point per line — leaving the radius to the pointer: the wedge leaves a
  one-parameter family of circles, so the figure would have to be guessed from whatever the pointer
  last stood on.
- A radius read as the pointer's distance to the *corner* (the centre standing on the bisector at that
  distance): the number typed would not be the radius, and the plate would name a length the circle
  does not have.
- Refusing a collinear third click inside `push`: `push` validates a figure only when it is complete,
  and a stage-local check would be the one refusal in the draft that is not a `path_with` answer.

## Measured after the work

- The figure is asserted three ways: the ring against a known right-angled corner and its opposite
  wedge (`crates/ui/src/draw_tests.rs`), the plate and the placed centre against a 45 degree wedge
  (`crates/ui/src/draw_fields.rs`), and a live frame that types the corner, both directions and the
  radius and reads the committed circle's world centre (`app/src/frame_tests.rs`).
- The lines are asserted through `paint_guides` and the frame's dashes (`app/src/draw_gizmo.rs`), and
  every stage's plates through `every_sketch_stage_draws_every_plate_apart`, which needed a fourth
  sample point for a four-click tool.
- The icon is measured by the footprint, coverage and README-table tests; the first drawing (a short
  V hugging the ring) was dropped after looking at it — at 26 px it read as a play button.
- Making the row live moved three ribbon tests off a "planned method" fixture (the pin is now drawn
  on the keyboard row of a four-method list) and the icon count to 114.
