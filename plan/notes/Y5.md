# Y5 — the camera gets a lens, and a sample lands on the aperture

The pinhole renderer this crate has been is sharp everywhere; the picture the owner points at is not.
The task adds depth of field: a lens radius, a focus distance, a sample on the aperture disc, and the
thin-lens ray that follows from them. Read with `plan/notes/Y1.md` and `Y2.md`: the estimator is
unchanged, only where its primary ray starts is.

## What was measured

- `crates/scene/src/camera.rs` had no lens field at all, and `crates/final/src/view.rs` built one ray
  per sample from the eye. `crates/render/src/scene.wgsl` is the *viewport* shader and stays out of
  this: the hint that named it is corrected in the map row, because a lens is the render's setting and
  the viewport keeps a pinhole.
- The sampler reserved its *second* pair for the eye in `DIMENSIONS`' own documentation ("the first
  pair jitters the pixel and the lens"), and nothing used it. The lens spends that pair, so the
  dimension budget did not grow: `DIMENSIONS` stays 8 and the sequence's first four dimensions keep
  their stratification.
- The thin lens, exactly: a point at distance `z` seen through a radius `a` focused at `F` is spread
  over `2 a |z - F| / F` at its own plane, which is the same disc at the focal plane scaled by the
  plane's own image scale. The unit test of the formula is exact; the device test measures a board's
  edge — a disc kernel ramps from a tenth to nine tenths of its level across `0.692` of its diameter —
  and reads the diameter within 2% of the formula (measured 15.8 px against 15.57 predicted for a
  100 mm lens at 2 m focus on a point at 1 m, a 852 mm field of view over 129 pixels).
- Convergence through a lens: the fixture at 256 samples per pixel is within 2% of its own 4096-sample
  reference (`a_shallow_focus_reaches_the_reference_noise_level_at_its_own_sample_count`), against 200
  samples for the pinhole image of Y2. A lens spends the second pair on a second draw, so the same
  count carries more variance and the number a person waits for moves from 200 to 256.
- A pinhole is bit-identical to the render that came before it: two runs of the fixture with a radius
  of zero and two different focus distances produce the same linear buffer, sample for sample.

## Decisions

- **The lens is the render's setting, not the document's.** `Settings::with_lens(Length, Length)`
  follows the studio's precedent (ADR 0213): the shell writes it over the copy of the camera the tracer
  reads, the Render place offers both numbers in millimetres, `z3l render` takes `--aperture` and
  `--focus`, and the schema does not move. The document camera gaining a lens would be its own
  decision, and its own schema bump.
- **`scene::Camera` carries the two numbers**, because the tracer reads a camera and not a setting: the
  radius and the focus travel with the view, default zero, and `with_lens` refuses the ends.
- **The concentric disc, and not a square scaled by a radius.** A square's corners are outside a lens
  and a naive scaling gathers points at the centre, which blurs the middle of the picture less than its
  edge. Shirley and Chiu's map is uniform by area, tested by the share of the samples in each half of
  the disc, and transcribed into the WGSL rather than decided twice.
- **An orthographic view has no lens.** Its rays have no eye to put a disc at, so `with_lens` is
  honoured in the fields and ignored by the ray: the uniform carries a switch, and the shader reads it
  once.

## Ruled out

- **An f-number instead of a radius.** It is the photographer's number, but the sampler needs the
  radius and the focal length the f-number divides by would be a second invention — this renderer has a
  field of view and no focal length, and inventing one to divide would be a conversion the maker
  cannot check against the picture.
- **Focusing on the orbit target automatically.** Tempting ("focus on what I am looking at"), but the
  target is view state and the setting is the render's; the default focus is the default camera's
  distance, which is the same number without the coupling.
- **Measuring the blur with a dot.** The first version rendered a 4 mm point and measured the spot's
  spread; the variance of "did this lens sample land on the dot" is enormous — a few hundred samples
  put the estimate 4-8% off and no more samples were affordable. A board's straight edge is a step
  whose ramp is the kernel, and forty rows averaged make the crossing steady.
- **Depth of field in the viewport.** It is a render's decision, taken once for an image; the viewport
  costs a frame every sixteen milliseconds and a lens there would blur what a person is modelling.
