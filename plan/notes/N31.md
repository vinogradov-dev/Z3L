# N31 — What was measured (2026-11-26)

## What was measured (2026-11-26)

The trim feature itself landed before this session, in `3315d72` (U124, ADR 0240): `PanelTrims` in
`crates/model/src/panel_features.rs`, the field on `Panel`, `panel.trim.set` in
`crates/engine/src/panel_cmds/panel.rs`, the four fields and the hint in
`crates/ui/src/panel_card.rs`, and the overlay front reading its lap through the same field. So N31
closes that feature out: the numbers exist where the gesture is, and four readers of the board still
describe it the old way.

Measured, one by one:

- **The mesh cache does not see the trims.** `PanelInputs` (`crates/mesh/src/lib.rs:421`) captures the
  outline, the nominal size, machining, banding, bevels and thickness, and `matches` compares the same
  list — no `trims`. `panel.trim.set` changes the ring the mesh is built from and leaves the key
  equal, so the viewport keeps the untrimmed board until something else changes. `HoleInputs`
  (`:551`) has the same gap, and the core box a hole is checked against moves with a low-edge trim.
- **The order package hands over the untrimmed outline.** `panel_facts`
  (`crates/export/src/rows.rs:264`) reads `PanelOutline::ring_and_sources_for`, which generates a
  rectangle with `PanelTrims::ZERO` (`crates/model/src/panel/contour.rs:649`), while the panel's own
  ring comes from `contour_with_sources_for`, which applies them. Measured on a 600 × 700 board with a
  2 mm bottom trim and a −14 mm top one: the parts row reads nominal 600 × 700, measured 600 × 712,
  and the `contours` table writes `0,0 → 600,0 → 600,700 → 0,700` — the frame, not the board a cutter
  would follow. The banding lengths come from that same ring.
- **The card has no manufactured size.** `show_resolved` (`crates/ui/src/panel_card.rs:784`) prints
  thickness, core and overall; the trims section prints four fields and a hint. Nothing says what the
  two numbers make of the board, which is the line the entry asks for.
- **`classify_outline` never sees a trimmed ring.** Its only two callers are the fill's Shape path
  (`crates/model/src/project/fill.rs:1268`, `:1294`), and both hand it the section ring of the niche —
  the frame — with the laps reported beside the plan (`Part::trims`). The classifier anchors a ring at
  the panel-local zero corner and refuses anything else, so a ring grown by a lap would not classify.
  The entry's sentence "unequal trims leave a trapezoid" does not describe the shipped design:
  ADR 0240 applies per-end insets to a rectangle, which stays a rectangle whatever the four numbers
  are, and the stored outline stays `PanelOutline::Rect` while the material moves inside or outside
  the frame. The Done clause is therefore met by one test over a plan with equal and unequal laps,
  not by a classifier change.
- **A ring can keep inert trims.** `convert_panel_to_contour`
  (`crates/model/src/project/panel_cmds.rs:206`) bakes the trim-aware ring and keeps the panel's
  `trims`, while a contour's ring generator ignores them (`ring_segments`), and `panel.trim.set`
  refuses a ring. The stored number would be a lie.
- **Resize keeps the trims today** (`set_panel_nominal_size` writes the size only), and the trim
  command already moves the end by the difference, so the clauses that read "one undo entry" and
  "a resize keeps the trims" need tests, not code.
- **The schema did not move.** `Panel::trims` is
  `#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = "PanelTrims::is_zero")]` and each of `PanelTrimsWire`'s
  four ends is `#[serde(default)]`, which is the compat-shaped default ADR 0096 forbids before the
  first release: a file may omit the field, or omit an end of it, and still load.

## The shape to build

1. `model`: one derivation of "what the board is", beside `nominal_size` — the ring's bounds as a
   `PanelNominalSize` — and one ring-with-sources accessor that reads the trims, so the exporter and
   the card cannot disagree with the panel.
2. `mesh`: the trims join `PanelInputs` and `HoleInputs`, so a trim rebuilds the board it changes.
3. `export`: the contours table and the banding lengths read the panel's own ring; the parts row keeps
   its nominal columns and its measured ones, which already carry the trim.
4. `ui`: a manufactured-size row in the trims section, beside the nominal fields.
5. `model`: a ring carries no trims — the conversion clears them and the loader refuses a ring that
   has one, so the number can never be stored and ignored.
6. `model`: the schema moves to 37, `Panel::trims` and the trim record become required in the file,
   and the goldens are rewritten in the same change.
7. Docs: `PROJECT.md`'s panel paragraph, `docs/domain/order-export.md` on the measured sizes and the
   outline, and `plan/map.md`'s row for the manufactured size.
## What landed, and what it measures now

- `Panel::manufactured_size` (the ring's bounds as a `PanelNominalSize`) and
  `Panel::contour_with_sources` (the ring with one source per segment) are the two questions the panel
  answers about its own board, and every reader goes through them.
- `PanelInputs` and `HoleInputs` carry the trims, so `panel.trim.set` rebuilds the board: the test
  `a_trim_moves_one_end_and_rebuilds_the_board_it_shortens` fails on the old key (checked by removing
  the comparison) and reads 500 mm then 514 mm of bounds against `manufactured_size`.
- The order package reads the panel's own ring: on a 600 × 700 board with 2 mm cut at the bottom and
  a 14 mm lap at the top, the parts row keeps 600 000 × 700 000 as the frame and reports 600 000 ×
  712 000 measured with an area of 600 × 712 mm², and the contour rows start 2 mm inside the frame and
  end 714 mm from it — the frame's own numbers before the change.
- The card prints `Made  {} × {}` under the four trim fields, drawn from `manufactured_size`, and the
  headless card test asserts the formatted row; a contour panel still reads its "no four ends"
  sentence instead of the fields and the row.
- A ring carries no trims: `convert_panel_to_contour` clears them after the bake (the bake already
  drew them, and the frame moves to the board so the world points stay), `set_panel_shape` refuses a
  ring with non-zero trims by name, and `PanelWire` refuses a stored pair. Schema 37 writes the four
  numbers on every panel and the trim record writes all four ends.
- `cargo xtask ci` is green on the last commit of the task.

## The one Done clause read, not guessed

"Two equal opposite trims classify as a rectangle, unequal ones leave a trapezoid the shape classifier
already knows" describes a design the tree does not have: ADR 0240 makes the trim a per-end inset of
the rectangle generator, which draws a rectangle for any four numbers, and the stored outline stays
`PanelOutline::Rect`. `classify_outline` never sees a trim — its only callers hand it the niche's
section ring, and the laps travel beside the plan — so the clause is met by a plan whose board stays a
rectangle whatever the laps are (`only_a_front_takes_a_mounting_and_an_inset_board_is_untouched`,
`a_part_standing_in_front_stops_the_lap_at_its_own_edge`). If the owner meant a classifier that
recovers a trimmed ring, that is a different capability and wants its own entry.
