# N33 — a niche keeps the frame's radii

what a session measured on the owner's scene, and how the section kept the curve without a new cell

## What was measured

- The owner's scene `/home/alx/skaf.z3l`: a product of 600 x 560 x 720 whose footprint is
  `Cut { corners: [None, None, None, Some(Radius { 200 })] }`, and one panel already created by the
  empty-space tool — 600 x 560 with `Chamfer { 200, 200 }` on the same corner. `Project::niche` answered
  that pentagon as the section at every height, so `classify_outline` read a chamfer and the panel
  stored one. The chord came from N29's `seed_polygon` (ADR 0201).
- `geom` already had the machinery: `Region` holds exact rings of lines and arcs, and
  `boolean::intersect_regions` is the boundary-of-the-set boolean that keeps them exact (ADR 0043). It
  had no half-plane cut, which is the only new primitive this task needed.
- The document's ring (`model::Contour`) holds arcs too, and `ContourSegment::from_geom` / `to_geom`
  convert both ways, so an arc of the frame can reach a panel's outline without a sample anywhere.
- The module doc of `Cell` said the free space is the intersection of wall *planes*, which is why the
  chord existed at all: a section is a ring, and only its walls are planes.
- Cost, measured on an empty 720-tall case with nothing in it (dev profile, opt-level 2, 200
  measurements): a 4/8/16/24-sided drawn ring 8.9/16.0/30.1/49.4 µs per niche, a rounded 600 x 560 case
  10.6 µs, the same case as a box 5.1 µs. The exact clip is one boolean against a four-corner covering
  rectangle per upright wall, so it is under a hundredth of a millisecond at its widest.

## Decisions

- **The cell keeps the ring beside its planes** (`Cell::ring`, plus the index where the planes a probe
  added begin). The planes stay the chords: they are what a probe meets, what the corner enumeration
  reads and what makes the largest fit safe. The ring is a second, exact reading of the same wall, and a
  clip never cuts it with the chords it seeded — that index is the seam (ADR 0202).
- **One prism section, clipped exactly.** `Cell::exact_section` walks the planes a probe added, skips the
  ones level with the section, and clips through the new `Region::clip_half_plane`. A box keeps the plane
  path, so nothing about a rectangular case moved; a shaped frame now answers a `Contour` with its arcs.
- **`FillMode::Shape` classifies the ring** (`exact_part`), so the panel is `Rect` with the frame's
  `CornerFeatureKind::Radius`, or a `Contour` with arcs where the section is not a rectangle. The ring is
  moved into the panel's plane once with `Contour::placed` — the stance's mirror and the move to the zero
  corner stay one rigid flat transform, so an arc stays an arc. Nothing is beveled: a cell of plumb walls
  has no tilted wall, so the far face is the base face.
- **The plan reports the polygon the ghost draws** — the same ring sampled at `FILL_SAMPLE_MM = 0.25`,
  the tolerance the shell already samples a frame's footprint at — while the outline it stores keeps the
  arc. That is the N17 rule for the frame, applied to the part.
- **Three limits, all of them existing rules.** A post or a wall stands *across* the cell, where the
  section is a projection of the ring and not the ring, so it keeps the plane reading. A hidden body
  trims the footprint by boxes, a polygon rule, so a trimmed part keeps the chord reading. And the largest
  fit still reads the chords, because a fit is a rectangle and its old answer is inside the new one.

## Ruled out

- A cell that holds arcs everywhere (`NichePlane` → a ring of walls): the corner enumeration, `cut` and
  the probes are plane algorithms and the curved-*body* debt entry is the same work; the section is the
  one place a curve is needed to answer a part, and clipping it costs a boolean.
- Sampling the ring for the section so that `classify_outline` would take a polygon: a sampled arc is
  many short lines and the classifier matches segment for segment, so the radius would have become a
  contour with a hundred segments — the same defect with more points.
- Re-fitting the panel that is already in the file: the fill creates the part a click would make; a
  command that re-shapes an existing panel against a new niche is a different tool and nothing asks for it.
- A second `FillPlan` field for the exact ring: the outline already is the ring, and the ghost needs the
  polygon; a third copy of the same shape is a third thing to keep in step.

## Verification

- Headless, geometry: `Region::clip_half_plane` has four tests — a cut that misses the ring hands it back
  segment for segment (an arc stays an arc), a cut through an arc keeps the shorter arc of the same
  centre and radius, a cut that leaves nothing answers `None`, and a plain rectangle clips to the
  rectangle beside the line.
- Headless, model: `a_rounded_corner_keeps_the_frames_own_radius` (five segments, one arc of 80 mm about
  (520, 80), the space between the chord and the arc is inside the section) and
  `a_wall_through_a_rounded_corner_cuts_the_arc_and_keeps_its_radius` (a panel's face at x = 560 trims the
  arc to its own radius and becomes the section's edge); `a_rounded_corner_stands_as_the_frames_own_radius`
  proves the Shape plan is `Rect` with `Radius { 80 }`, that the created panel stores it, and that every
  point of the stored ring lies inside the frame.
- On the owner's file, a scratch probe (`crates/project`, deleted): every height answers
  `Rect { corner_features: [Radius { 200000 }] }` with 20 sampled ghost points and none outside the frame.
- Not measured in the window: the ghost's arc is the sampled polygon, drawn by the shell's existing
  wireframe path with no code change, and the panel's own arc is derived where the mesh is built.
