# U116 — the snaps are one set, resolved in the model

## What was measured

- `app/src/gizmo_math.rs` held a plane-local resolver (`snap_point`, `SnapRequest`, `SnappedPoint`,
  `SnapTarget`): draft ends, a Shift axis lock and the 10 mm grid, all in work-plane millimetres and
  blind to the document. `model::SnapSettings` held a second one — the coordinate planes, the
  neighbour frames and a tolerance — read by `Project::move_rule` and nothing else.
- The grid's switch lived in `DrawGizmo` (`grid`, `toggle_grid`), so it was a viewport's taste:
  `G` reached it only while a draft or the ruler ran (`Document::toggle_draw_grid`), the ribbon cell
  `view.snaps.grid` read it, and no workspace file carried it.
- The ruler had its own surface rule (`measure_gizmo::surface_under`): the probe from the eye,
  restarting a micrometre inside each product envelope, at most four shells, so a person pointing at a
  board behind the reserved box measures the board.
- The fill and the niche ghost never resolved a point of their own: they read `ui::pointer_point`,
  which is the niche atom's cursor rule inside the open product.
- The View tab's Snaps group was one cell (`view.snaps.grid`, icon `view/grid-snap`), and
  `cargo xtask ui-rules` fails a live ribbon cell without artwork, so a set of cells is a set of
  drawings.
- `egui 0.37`'s test driver is `Context::run_ui`, and a `TexturesDelta` must be cleared before it is
  dropped or the test panics; both were found by the marker test rather than by reading.

## Decisions

- **The atom lives in the model** (`crates/model/src/project/snap.rs`), although the `files:` hint
  named only `app/src/gizmo_math.rs`. The candidates are document queries — a panel's nominal box and
  drawn ring, an object's profile or mesh bounds, a container's envelope — and writing that walk in
  the app would have been the second copy the task exists to delete (ADR 0199).
- **Ten kinds, one order.** `SnapKind` derives its `Ord` from the declaration order, so the priority
  is data: a corner beats a nearer edge, and the nearest candidate inside one kind wins.
- **The grid moved into the workspace settings.** `SnapSettings::kinds` (screen: `PointerKinds` +
  `planes`) is one value with ten switches; `Document` keeps the live copy, the shell writes the file,
  and `G` now runs with no tool armed because the switch is the workspace's. The old file keys
  (`planes`, `frames`, `tolerance_mm`, `floor`) stay where they were and `kinds` defaults every field
  to on, so a workspace written before the section keeps its own choices.
- **A face is the tool's reading, not the atom's probe.** The ruler hands in the face it measured;
  the atom ranks it and uses it as the eye's limit, so a corner *behind* that face does not capture.
  The drawing tools hand in nothing, because their point is the candidate's footprint on the plane.
- **The centre kind is a surface's middle.** A panel or a body offers the six face centres and not
  the middle of its box: the ruler pointed at a demo panel first captured the box centre, which is
  inside the board and failed the "the point is on a face" assertion of `the_ruler_snaps_to_the_board`.
  A primitive's own centre and a product's envelope centre stay, because neither is inside material.
- **The move consumes the kinds through `snap_distance`.** Only `centre` adds a capture there: the
  middle of a shell's reserved space, which is the coordinate a box's faces do not offer. A body's
  middle is inside the body, where contact keeps a part out, so the capture is gated on `is_shell`.
- **`MeasureGizmo::update` bundles the camera and the rectangle** into `MeasureInput`: it had nine
  arguments and clippy's `too_many_arguments` allow; the eighth value entered the struct, as
  AGENTS §1.5 says, and the allow is gone.
- **Nine drawings, one family.** The new cells use the `reference` ink with a `view`-ink control
  point, like `view/grid-snap`; the grid cell keeps that drawing and gains no tenth file.

## Ruled out

- Occlusion by probing from the atom: it would add a whole-document probe to every sketch frame, and
  the face the tool already measured answers the same question for the one tool that stores the point.
- Moving the fill's point through the resolver: the niche is a volume, and a snap of a few
  millimetres inside the same cell changes nothing a maker can see (recorded in `plan/debt.md`).
- One cell with ten variants (a list under the cell): the task asks for a toggle per kind in the
  group, and a list hides nine switches behind one click.
- Reusing the old `SnapTarget` in the app and mapping it onto the kinds: two enums, one meaning.
- A subscription model (the tab pushes into the settings): the shell already collects requests from
  the tab (`take_mode_request`, `take_snaps_change`), so the snaps took the same road.

## The owner's report (T17)

A drawn cube stuck only to the contour it was drawn from, the marker stood on the base, and a ball
stuck nowhere. Three causes, all measured after the report:

- `nearest_point_to_ray` compared the distance to the ray alone, so a box's upper and lower corner —
  one line through the eye — were a tie the document's order broke, and the lower one won. Depth now
  breaks the tie, and a test asks a board from above and from below.
- The reach was one world distance measured at the plane; a candidate far behind it was captured from
  19 mm away, and `Vertex` outranks `Centre`, so a corner 19 mm off beat the centre under the pointer.
  `SnapRequest::tolerance_depth` carries the depth the reach was measured at and the resolver scales
  it per candidate — a screen radius, not a world distance.
- The drawing tool projected the marker from the stored plane point, so a candidate above the plane
  drew its marker on the base contour. The marker now stands on the candidate, and a typed number is
  the one thing that moves it back to the plane.
- A ball answers at its own centre and at its six quadrants: a sphere has no corner and no edge, and
  the six box-face centres are the places it does have.
