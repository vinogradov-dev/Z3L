# X77 — the move answers the destination, and a carcass is a body to what is not its pie

the move answers the destination, and a carcass is a body to what is not its piece

## What was measured

- All three reported symptoms came from one decision: the rule answered the path. `stop_at_contact`
  cut the travel at the first contact along the drag measured from the start boxes, so a free place
  beyond an obstacle was unreachable by construction, whatever the pointer named.
- The per-axis answers were each exact and still combined into a placement inside a turned neighbour
  (each axis measured with the other two at zero). That is the perspective/axonometry penetration:
  the destination was never tested, only the three paths.
- A shell and a body were skipped as a pair in `stop_at_contact` *and* in `resting_inside`, because a
  shelf in a case is a body in a shell. The skip was written for containment and silently covered
  every carcass-vs-solid pair, which is why the cabinet passed through a solid body and the body
  through the cabinet.
- The old test `a_turned_product_is_measured_by_its_world_box` passed on floating noise: the turned
  envelope's y max was 1.24e-13 and the strict `overlap` test read it as an overlap. Fixed by moving
  the neighbour into the turned frame's real reach, not by loosening the test.

## Decisions

- The destination is answered once for the whole vector: free → taken whole (the jump), blocked →
  `resolve` in world-axis order, each axis decided at the position the previous axes produced, cut at
  the first contact from there. An axis whose cut still cannot clear the pair keeps the piece where it
  stood; the final placement is always one the guard accepts. 0166.
- Pair rule: body↔body, shell↔shell, shell↔body all measured. Exempt only a legitimate nesting — a
  node that is a child of the case whose shell contains it, and a shell strictly inside a shell. A
  body *standing* inside a cabinet's footprint is not a piece of it. The guard reads the same rule.
- "Already overlapping is no new overlap" stays, as ADR 0162 wrote it: a pair the document already
  holds can be dragged out and is never frozen. That also means a post whose nominal box intersects a
  shelf is still unpoliced — the box is not the board (a notch is machining, not geometry), and the
  honest fix is contact against the panel outline, not a stricter guard.
- Cross-axis gaps below `CONTACT_MM` (1 µm) are treated as contact, not as an axis that never closes:
  two turned boxes produce exactly that noise on the edge-edge axes, and reading it as "never
  touches" reported no contact for a pair that was in the way.

## Tests and their numbers

- Two 100³ bodies 200 mm apart: a 250 mm drag is cut at 200 with the contact; a 1000 mm drag is taken
  whole and lands beyond.
- A turned 200 x 20 x 10 board against a 100³ neighbour: a destination inside the neighbour is cut at
  400 (the board's own corner), not at the 358.58 of its world box; a destination beyond it lands.
- A body's 100 mm destination inside a cabinet's envelope stops at its face; a shelf inside its own
  case moves freely inside it; a unit inside a niche stops at the ceiling when the destination
  straddles it and lands on the outer's top when the destination is beyond it.
- The diagonal corner case asserts both ends: the hand's own diagonal is refused by the guard, and the
  rule's preview is accepted.
- The app's `both_moves_answer_the_place_the_board_lands...`: a 60-point drag stops the divider flush
  on its neighbour at 1068, a 400-point drag lands it beyond 1668, one undo entry each, both tools.

## Ruled out

- Sweeping the whole path *and* allowing jumps: a swept test plus a destination test is two rules with
  two answers; the destination alone is what the owner described.
- Freezing a pair that already overlaps (the post through shelves case): it would freeze every part of
  a real cabinet whose box intersects a shelf that is notched around it.
- Keeping the shell/body skip and only fixing the drag: the write's guard would still accept a body
  left inside a carcass, so the next drag would find the pair "already overlapping" and never police
  it again.
