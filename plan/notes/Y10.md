# Y10 — a frosted pane is the roughness a pane already had

a frosted pane is the roughness a pane already had

## What was measured

- `crates/final/src/bsdf.rs` `sample`: a pane's transmission was the smooth `refract(wo, ratio)`,
  weighted `transmission · ratio²`. `Material::roughness` reached the reflection lobe and nothing
  else, so every pane in a picture was clear float glass.
- `crates/scene/src/lib.rs` `Surface::roughness` and `app/src/scene_build.rs::paint_of` already carry
  a record's roughness to a glass front, so a frosted pane needed no new field, no wire change and no
  UI: a glass record with a roughness is a sandblasted one.
- The pane split is `probabilities`: `specular = F(cos_o)`, `transmitted = 1 − F(cos_o)`, one Fresnel
  for the whole event (ADR 0243). It is unchanged.
- Transmission directions against the visible-normal density (`transmitted_normal_cdf`, quadrature
  over the half vectors), roughness 0.1 / 0.5 / 1.0: the sampler's share inside 1/2/4/8 widths of
  `alpha` agrees with the lobe's own to within 1.0 % at every width, and the beam's rms deviation from
  the smooth refraction is `0.0122 / 0.1686 / 0.3742` rad.
- Energy, `R + T / eta²` at `cos_o = 0.8`: `1.0000` at roughness 0 and 0.1, `0.9851` at 0.5,
  `0.8326` at 1.0; at `cos_o = 0.2` and roughness 0.5, `0.9150`. The sampler agrees with the
  half-vector quadrature within 0.25 %. What is lost is the single-scattering masking loss, the same
  one the conductor's completion pays for: a frosted pane is not energy-completed in this entry.
- The transmitted density's mass over the lower hemisphere is `1.0000` at roughness 0.5 and 1.0
  (1024² and 2048² grids), which is exactly the share of the transmission draws that cross.
- Device, a bar behind a 400 mm slab at 40° and a uniform white room, 96 × 72: the bar above 15 % of
  the room covers 6.2 % of the picture at roughness 0 and 14.2 % at 0.6. The mean distance from the
  2048-sample picture at 64 samples is 0.0259 and 0.0651; at 16/64/256/1024 samples the frosted frame
  reads 0.0895 / 0.0651 / 0.0403 / 0.0209 against the clear frame's
  0.0545 / 0.0259 / 0.0127 / 0.0048. A frosted pane therefore needs about four times the samples for
  the same distance, and a 1024-sample picture is within 2 % of its reference.
- Caustics: one pixel of the 2048-sample frosted picture reads 54 against the room's 1.0. They are
  found by chance and not sampled, as the entry says, which is why the noise is measured as a mean
  distance rather than an rms.

## Decisions

- **The transmitted beam is drawn about the visible normal the reflection lobe already draws**, by
  Walter et al. 2007 §4: `sample_visible_normal`, then `refract_about(wo, h, eta)`. One drawer serves
  both lobes, the sampler is not widened, and a rough pane costs one `sample_visible_normal` per
  crossing.
- **`f |cos| / pdf` cancels the Jacobian, so the weight is `(1 − F(h)) / (1 − F(cos_o)) · eta² ·
  G2 / G1`.** The refracted direction's density is the visible-normal density times
  `eta² |wi · h| / |wo + eta wi|²`; the BSDF carries the same factor and the `eta²` radiance gain. The
  half-angle Fresnel over the split's own is what makes `R + T / eta²` come out at the masking
  albedo exactly, and it is the same choice the pane's *reflection* lobe already made — its `eval`
  reads `F(wo · h)`, so a transmission that read `1 − F(cos_o)` would double-count the difference.
  Measured over the half vectors: weighting the transmission by the split's own Fresnel would
  return 1.3 % more light than the pane is given at roughness 1.
- **A roughness of zero keeps today's `refract`, bit for bit.** The entry asks for it and it is also
  the cheapest guarantee that no clear pane in any existing picture moved: the smooth branch is
  entered before a normal is drawn. `MIN_ROUGHNESS` would have jittered the beam by a milliradian.
- **`lambda` takes the magnitude of its cosine.** The Smith term is a function of the angle from the
  normal, and a transmitted direction arrives with a negative z; clamping the signed cosine turned
  `G2` into `1/500` and quietly killed a frosted pane's light. The signature is unchanged and every
  existing caller passes a positive cosine.
- **The transmissive half vector is not `normalize(wo + eta wi)` whichever sign faces up.** Entering a
  denser medium it is the negation of that sum, leaving it the sum itself; choosing by `z > 0` answers
  the reflection-like root for a beam that never crossed anything, and a density that counts those
  directions over-counted the transmitted mass by 22 % at roughness 1.

## Ruled out

- **Adding the transmitted lobe to `eval` and `pdf`.** A light sample is a direction towards an
  emitter, and the lobe lives below the surface: no caller evaluates it, and the density a sample is
  drawn with is already `Scatter::pdf`. It would be a second transcription in Rust and WGSL with no
  reader.
- **A white-furnace completion for the dielectric**, the way the conductor's multiple-scattering term
  works. It is a second measured table and a second lobe for a loss that only bites at roughness 1,
  and no sandblasted front in a picture is that rough; recorded here as the gap Y10 leaves.
- **A new document or catalogue field named "frosted".** The roughness a glass record already stores
  is the whole of it: a coating word would be a second number for one surface and a wire bump.
