# X62 — the ground shadow is cast by the light, not drawn under the body

## What was measured

`target/ui-shots/probe-shadow-on.png` and `probe-shadow-off.png` (before this task) were identical
pixel for pixel, so the shipped ellipse drew nothing a person could see. Three independent reasons,
each enough on its own:

- **Geometry.** The ellipse covered 92 % of the body's footprint while the body stood on the same
  plane, so the body's own silhouette covered every pixel of it. The pixels it ever owned were those
  seen through the sub-millimetre gap `FLOOR_TOLERANCE_MM` allowed: at 0.4 mm off the floor a shadow
  appeared, at 0.6 mm it was gone.
- **Applicability.** It needed a body resting on `z = 0`, and almost nothing in furniture does.
- **Contrast.** Black at 40 % over `0.10, 0.11, 0.13` is no shadow. At 1.6× the footprint and full
  strength it was still invisible on the dark theme; the same probe on the light theme read well
  (`probe-light-on.png`), which is what separated "wrong shape" from "wrong background".

## What was decided

ADR 0142 replaces 0071. A shadow is the silhouette the key light casts on a **floor plate**, not a
footprint under a body:

- The projection is one rule — a point at height `z` lands at `xy - light.xy * z / light.z` — so no
  path asks whether a body touches the floor (X62's own defect, the box of the turned box, closes by
  construction: nothing is derived from a bound any more).
- The mask is a *coverage* render, not a depth map: the receiver is one plane, so there is nothing to
  compare and no bias, acne or peter-panning to tune. 1024², `R8Unorm`, 1 MB, over the plate's square.
- Two separable blur passes give the penumbra a world size (60 mm = about 6 texels), so the softness
  does not change with the zoom. The frame pays one texture fetch in the plate's fragment.
- The plate is `SceneColors::floor`, single-sided (a view from below keeps the cabinet), drawn first
  in each view's pass in every display style.
- The mask is keyed by `GroundKey` (upload generation, quantised light direction, plate half-size):
  an edit or a turned sun rebuilds it, an orbit does not. The ground's uniform slot is one past the
  views in the camera buffer, so the mask pass binds the same layout the views do.

## What was ruled out

- Keeping the ellipse and widening it past the footprint: it still needs floor contact, cannot speak
  about a hanging body, and would be a fuzzy ring on a background that cannot darken.
- A 13-tap blur in the plate's fragment: at 10 mm per texel that reaches ±10 mm, nowhere near a
  penumbra, and a wider kernel costs every plate fragment on every frame.
- An infinite ground plane: the grid's square is where the lines end and where the mask ends, and a
  scene fitted to a cabinet never sees the far edge.
- Casting shadow on the panels in the same change: that is a depth map with a bias, a different size
  of work, and it is scheduled as `L1`.

## What was measured after

`probe-ground-dark.png` and `probe-ground-light.png` (both themes, at 40 % strength): the shadow is a
soft band beside the panel, offset by the sun, 28 660 pixels on the dark theme against the same frame
with the strength at zero. The plate reads as its own surface — 43, 46, 54 against the 26, 28, 33
background. On the light theme the plate is `0.93` paper and the shadow reads as a pencil smear.
