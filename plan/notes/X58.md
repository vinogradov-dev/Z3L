# X58 — Culling a frame's drawables by the view volume the camera sees

## What was measured

- `crates/render/src/lib.rs`, `ViewportRenderer::upload`: `draw_order` was filled with the whole
  index range `0..drawables.len()`, and every pass — opaque faces, the translucent order, the
  visible-edge pass and the stencil-gated hidden-edge pass — walked all of it. Nothing read the
  camera before binding a buffer.
- The frame already carried the value a cull needs: `NodeGeometry::body_bounds` and
  `band_bounds` hold the box the mesh cache measured (X56), and `app/src/scene_build.rs` already
  turns its eight corners into world space for the scene bounds. The drawable itself did not carry
  it, so the renderer had no box to test.
- The entry's own measurement: 500 / 2 000 / 5 000 spheres are 0.48 M / 1.9 M / 4.8 M triangles and
  9.5 ms of an empty frame at the top count, all of it spent whether or not the sphere is on screen.

## Decisions

- The box lives on the drawable as `Drawable::local_bounds` (the mesh's own frame) and
  `Drawable::world_bounds()` derives the world box from the transform. Storing the *world* box
  would go stale the moment a caller moves `world_transform`, which tests do; eight corner
  transforms per drawable per upload cost nothing beside the upload itself.
- `Drawable::with_box` is the evaluation path: the scene walk hands over the cached box, so a
  rebuild never measures a mesh. `Drawable::new` still measures, for a hand-built mesh in a test or
  the on-screen display, and its doc says which one the frame build uses.
- `Frustum::of_camera(camera, aspect)` reads the six planes out of `Camera::view_projection`, the
  same matrix the shader receives. One rule then covers perspective and orthographic, and the
  depth range difference (`0..1` here, `-1..1` in the textbook) is one row: the near plane is the
  `z` row, not `w + z`.
- A box is dropped only when it lies wholly outside one plane (its farthest corner along that
  plane's normal is outside). A box straddling the near plane therefore stays: part of it can still
  reach into the picture.
- `Frustum::visible` fills one `Vec<usize>` the caller owns, and every pass of a view reads that
  same list, so a culled drawable loses its faces and its edges together and the pass loop never
  allocates. The list is rebuilt per view: two views see one frame from different sides.
- The renderer culls over the drawables it uploaded, not over `frame.drawables()`: the buffers are
  what a pass draws, and a frame handed to `render` that was never uploaded must not index them.
- `ViewportRenderer::drawn_drawables()` exposes the count after culling — the number a test asserts
  and a cost readout can print.
- Verified offscreen on a real adapter: two wireframe drawables, one in view and one 50 m below it,
  draw one drawable (362 edge pixels) and leave the lower half of the target empty.

## Ruled out

- Measuring the box at upload from `mesh.local_bounds()`: O(vertices) per upload, which is the cost
  X56 removed from the same walk.
- Computing the visible set in `scene_build` and carrying it in the frame: the set depends on the
  camera and the aspect of each view, and a camera move must not rebuild the frame.
- A second pass over the frame in `render_views` that filters the uploaded buffers by index into
  `frame.drawables()`: two lists to keep aligned, and an index from one frame into another's
  buffers is exactly the bug the stencil slots avoid by indexing one list.
- Oriented boxes per drawable: a conservative world box never hides a body that is in view, and the
  tight box of a turned panel would cost a second cache (X62 keeps the shadow of a turned body).
