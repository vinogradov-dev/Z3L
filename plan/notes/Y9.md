# Y9 — What was measured

## What was measured

- `crates/final/src/bsdf.rs` `probabilities`: `diffuse = 1 - metallic`, `specular = metal · (1 -
  multiple)`, `completion = metal · multiple`. At `metallic = 0` an opaque surface has no specular
  lobe at all, so a lacquered board was a matte rectangle with a picture on it.
- `crates/final/src/gpu/layout.rs`: `GpuFace` is nine `vec4`s, 144 bytes, and six of its channels
  were written as zero (`colour.w`, `tint.w`, `uv_b.z`, `uv_b.w`, `decor.y/z/w`).
- `crates/materials/src/material.rs`: `Coat` already stored a weight, a gloss in percent and its own
  ior, and `app/src/scene_build.rs::paint_of` read `record.base()` and dropped `record.coat()`.

## Decisions

- **One Fresnel for the whole event, read at the view angle.** The layer reflects
  `weight · F(cos_o)` and the base gets `1 - that`, which is the number ADR 0243 writes as the
  transmittance, and the coat's own GGX lobe is weighted by the same constant. Two consequences
  measured below: the estimator conserves energy by construction, and a coat draw's weight is the
  microfacet's own `G2 / G1`, so a 90 % gloss draws no fireflies.
- **The gloss becomes a width once**, in `scene::Coat::glossed`, at the only place a record turns into
  a frame: `alpha = (1 - gloss / 100)²`, V-Ray's and Cycles' mapping (ADR 0243). 90 % gloss is
  `alpha = 0.01`, the tracer clamps every width to `MIN_ROUGHNESS`.
- **The layer costs the face no byte**: weight in `colour.w`, alpha in `tint.w`, ior in `uv_b.z`, so a
  `GpuFace` stays 144 bytes and one memory sector (`docs/performance.md` §2).
- **A pane is never a coat** (ADR 0243): `is_pane` returns before either reaches the coat, so a glass
  front keeps the transmissive path it had and Y10 scatters what it transmits.
- **A record with no base still paints the placeholder.** A layer has nothing to be seen through, and
  the composition of a veneer and a lacquer is the catalogue's (O18), not the frame's.

## Ruled out

- **Fresnel at the half angle.** The microfacet value `F(wo·h)` inside the coat's lobe, with the base's
  transmittance still `1 - F(cos_o)`. Measured on a white board (albedo 1) with a full 50 % gloss coat:
  the coat's own albedo falls to `0.172` at `cos_o = 0.1` (the lobe's average Fresnel over a rough
  layer is 0.19, not the 0.57 that `F(cos_o)` gives), so the summed model answered `0.60` of the board
  it was made of — nearly half the energy gone at grazing incidence. With one constant Fresnel, the
  same surface answers `0.94` at 0.1, `0.986` at 0.5 and `0.997` at 1.0.
- **An energy completion for the coat**, like the conductor's: at gloss 20·(70·(99 the coat's own
  single-scattering albedo is `0.833 / 0.62 / 1.0` of what a reflectance of one would return, and the
  clause the entry owes is a model that answers at least nine tenths of its albedo, which it does.
  A completion is a second measured table for one lost tenth.
- **A second drawable or a fifth surface kind** for a finished board: the layer is a field of the
  surface the base already is, so a lacquered veneer stays one triangle set and one record.

## What the session that built it found

- **The quadrature has to follow the width.** A grid uniform over the hemisphere misses a 99 % gloss
  lobe entirely (a tenth of a milliradian wide). The test lays its grid out in the half vector's own
  tangent angle, one `alpha` per step, out to fifty widths; run with a reflectance of one against the
  conductor's measured energy table, that quadrature agrees within 0.4 % at `cos 0.1/0.5/1.0`, which
  is what makes it an independent reading rather than the sampler with a second name.
- **Sampler and quadrature agree within two percent of the value** at gloss 99 / 70 / 20 and
  `cos 0.1/0.5/1.0` on a black base; the measured gaps were inside half a percent and the tolerance is
  the sampler's own standard error at 65 536 samples, not a number chosen to pass.
- **A weight of zero is bit-identical**, and the test proves it by pairing the material with a coat of
  weight zero whose alpha and ior are nonsense (`0`, `NaN`, `∞`): the scatters, the probabilities, the
  evaluations and the densities all come out equal to the bits, so the layer is never read.
- **The device sees the sheen.** Two white spheres, one lamp mirrored about a normal 75° off the view:
  the rim band's brightest pixel reads `0.395` on the matte sphere and `20.09` on the 90 % lacquered
  one, and the axis of both reads `0.25` — the room's own radiance — because a near-mirror layer over
  a board in a uniform room returns exactly what the board did.
- **The coat's draw share is its reflectance, not its energy.** At `cos_o = 0.1` a full coat is drawn
  57 times in a hundred and the base 43, which is the split the transmittance names; the weights are
  `G2 / G1` and the estimate is unbiased, which is what the sampler-versus-quadrature test measures.
