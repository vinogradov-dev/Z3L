# X67 / X68 — a camera change keeps the piece and returns the pointer to it

## What the owner reported

"The panel moves, then I switch to the front view and the panel has already flown away. Then I press
Home and the panel is far away. In perspective the problem is still there, and in axonometric it is
there too: when you zoom in or out the piece flies away from the cursor."

## What was measured (frame tests, no guessing)

- The carry itself is 1:1 in every projection: hand steps of 20/40/60 points carried the pivot 19.7 /
  39.1 / 57.9 points, and a purely sideways hand moved nothing. The travel rule of ADR 0153 holds.
- A wheel zoom under the carry separated the piece from the hand: three steps at 0.8 (a 2× zoom) left
  it 140 points away. The cause is that `Camera::zoom` scales about the *target*, the view's centre,
  so everything the maker looks at slides away from the pointer.
- A *Front* preset (direction plus orthographic) and Home (a refit) moved the piece by 100 points and
  more on screen, in whichever direction the new projection put it.
- The gaps 600/600 in both screenshots were not a pin: typing digits while a piece is carried does not
  reach a field (measured), and a pin needs a typed number, a lock or the centre button. They are the
  live gaps of a piece that had been left 600 from either face.

## Decisions

- The wheel zooms towards the pointer (ADR 0154): the point under it keeps its screen place, in both
  projections, and the two drags that zoom go through the same function.
- A carried piece keeps the screen place the hand gave it (ADR 0155): on every camera change the piece
  slides along its own axis until its projected pivot is where it was. One first-order step per
  iteration with the axis' own scale, eight at most, and an axis that points at the camera keeps what
  it has.

## Ruled out

- Leaving the piece at its world place and drawing a tether to the cursor: the owner reads the
  separation as the defect, and a tether explains a bug instead of fixing it.
- Releasing the carry on a camera change: N25 settled that the view moves under the piece, and the
  owner zooms while carrying on purpose.
- Centring the zoom on the carried piece instead of the pointer: the pointer is where the maker looks,
  and a zoom that ignored it would surprise every other gesture.
- Correcting the share across the piece's axis: a panel only rises and falls in its thickness
  direction, and a carry that could move it sideways would break the document's own move rule.

## The third pass: the owner's own sequence, and what it settled

The owner then described the sequence exactly: *take a panel, put it 200 mm from its neighbour, pan
the view a long way with the right button, come back to the carry* — and the panel was far from the
pointer. His rule: a view gesture must not change the panel's position, so the **cursor** has to come
back to the panel; and while the view is being turned the cursor should be hidden.

Measured in a frame test (`a_pan_under_a_carried_piece_leaves_the_piece_where_it_was`): a 420-point
right-button pan left the pointer 52 points off the piece, and the piece's screen place slid 320
points because the pan drags the world under the pointer while the piece sits at another depth.

Both earlier answers were wrong the same way: they attached the *piece* to the pointer. The carry now
holds the piece through a gesture (nothing moves) and asks the *window* to put the pointer back on it
afterwards (`ViewportCommand::CursorPosition`), with the cursor hidden for the length of the gesture.
A window that cannot move the pointer (Wayland) falls back to carrying on from where the pointer is,
without a jump. ADR 0156 replaces ADR 0155.

Two defects found while building it: the grab offset must be read at the **click** and not when the
axis is named (the hand has already travelled `NAMING_TRAVEL` by then, and the offset swallowed it,
so the piece never moved), and the home request is `pivot − offset`, not `pivot + offset`.

## The fourth pass: the owner said nothing had changed

He was right, and the binary was newer than the commit, so it was the code. Three defects:

1. **The pointer was never hidden.** `Document::carry_cursor` read `navigating` — the document's
   *armed navigation mode*, which is off for a right-button pan — instead of the input of a camera
   move. Now the gesture is the input itself (`pointer.middle_down() || secondary_down() ||
   smooth_scroll_delta != 0`), and `CursorVisible` is re-sent every frame the pointer stays hidden.
2. **A gesture never asked for the pointer back.** The request was gated on `camera_moved`, which is
   only true on the frame the camera changed — and on exactly those frames `navigating` was true, so
   the request was refused. The gizmo now keeps `moved_by_gesture` and asks on the first frame after
   the gesture ends.
3. **The home point ignored the carry.** `world_box` is the document's box; the piece in the hand has
   moved by `drag.delta`, so the pointer flew to where the piece stood when it was taken. The middle
   is `box centre + drag.delta`.

The owner's own words for the target — "the centre of the front edge of the panel" — are read as the
middle of the piece's box, which is the point a maker reaches for; the pivot is a panel's base
corner, and a click point cannot be found again.

Test `a_pan_under_a_carried_piece_leaves_the_piece_where_it_was` now checks all three: the pointer is
hidden mid-pan (`CursorHidden(false)` in the frame's commands), the board's `world_placement` does not
change, and on release the pointer lands in the middle of the board and the hand carries again.

## The fifth pass: the session is Wayland, and the pointer cannot be moved at all

The owner reported "nothing changed, and worse". The cause was the mechanism, not a bug in it: this
desktop is Wayland (`XDG_SESSION_TYPE=wayland`, `WAYLAND_DISPLAY=wayland-1`), and `winit`'s
`set_cursor_position` answers `NotSupported` there unless the pointer is *locked*
(`wayland/window/state.rs`). `ViewportCommand::CursorPosition` was therefore dropped on the floor, and
`CursorVisible` was undone by `egui-winit`, which re-shows the cursor on every icon change — and the
icon changes every frame the scene is drawn. That is why three rounds of fixes looked like nothing.

The carry now keeps its own hand, which is what the app already does with its pointer: the work area
draws a square and hides the system arrow (X40). So the hand is the square:

- the system pointer while it tracks the mouse (ordinary carrying, unchanged);
- hidden for the length of a camera gesture (nothing is drawn, no request to the window);
- put on the middle of the piece on the first frame after the camera moved, with the bond re-read
  there so that frame carries nothing;
- moved by the mouse's own *movement* from then on, so dragging carries the piece from the piece.

The distance card hangs from the hand too, and the piece is held on the re-home frame (`rehome` is
checked before the solve), which is the bug that made the first try move the piece on a preset.
