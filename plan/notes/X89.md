# X89 — a scene with a hole, and why it is a ring mesh

## What was measured

- The workspace has no boolean operations in the scene, so a hole is **authoring data**: a plate
  whose top and bottom are three quads around a square opening, its four hole walls and its four
  outer walls. `Mesh::new` takes polygon faces and the shape builder fans them into triangles, so the
  fixture needs no new geometry code.
- The plate's own shape is a **triangle surface** and not a hull: the convexity test in
  `project::contact_shape` sees the hole's wall planes with the outer rim in front of them, so the
  hole stays a hole. A hull would have filled it and the ball would have stopped on nothing visible.
- The **floor** answers before any body does. A ball dropped straight down a hole at z = 0 is stopped
  by the workspace's floor at 0, not by the plate, so the scene stands its plate at z = 200: the ball
  then falls through the hole and stops on the floor, which is the honest demonstration of both the
  port and ADR 0177.
- The two readings in one gesture: with the adapter the drag answers −320 mm (through the hole, down
  to the floor), without it −115 mm (the box around the plate, ball on the plate's top face). The
  delta between the answers is what X85 was for.

## Decisions

- The scene is written from the model API by an `#[ignore]`d test and read back by an ordinary one:
  the committed JSON is exactly what the builder produces, and a change to the fixture without
  regenerating the file fails the ordinary test.
- `SceneFile::from_document` is a small public addition: a fixture or a tool writes the same envelope
  the editor writes (one format, one version, no preserved sections).
- The plate stands above the floor rather than the scene depending on a workspace switch: a person
  opening the file sees the ball pass through the hole without changing a setting.

## Ruled out

- **A panel with a hole** — panels carry machining in their own frame and no through-cut yet; a ring
  mesh is the honest representation today.
- **A boolean in the fixture** — the kernel has one, but the *scene* has no way to author it, and a
  fixture that needs a command the UI cannot run is not a scene the owner can inspect.
- **Ball at z = 150 over a plate at z = 0** — the floor stops the ball inside the plate's thickness,
  which reads as a bug in the port until the plate is raised.
