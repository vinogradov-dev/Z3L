# N24 — the furniture move takes the piece by a click and follows the pointer

## What was measured

- `translate_gizmo.rs` held one gesture for both tools: `hit_axis` over `HandleSpec`, a press that
  built `Drag`, a per-frame `closest_parameter_on_line`, and `!down` as the commit. Everything but
  the press and the commit is the distance maths, and it is the same maths a carried piece needs.
- `Project::furniture_axes` answers per-selection axes for any node, while `Project::move_rule`
  answers `None` outside furniture (`is_furniture` = a product or a part inside one). The two were
  already read separately, so a loose board keeps its one axis and loses only the rule.
- `MoveRule::limit(axis, proposed)` already zeroes an axis the drag does not offer, so the tool never
  needs its own "may this axis move" branch.
- `axis_handles` projects each allowed axis at the pivot and skips the ones within 10° of the view
  direction (`VIEW_ALIGNMENT_LIMIT = 0.985`). The unit direction it returns is exactly what a travel
  test needs, so `named_axis` shares the projection instead of a second one.
- The viewport's click block runs *after* the gizmo's `update` and is gated by `consumed_click`, and
  `document.rs` already computes `capturing` from `is_dragging()`, so a held piece keeps the pointer
  over chrome and freezes the camera like any other drag.
- `hover()` in the frames moves the pointer and settles without a button; `click()` is move + press +
  release + settle. The furniture gesture is therefore two `click`s with a `hover` between them.
- A frame test that clicks a part of a **closed** product selects the product, not the part: the
  fixture has to `open_product` before the body-grab reads as a part (the old handle test never
  noticed because it set the selection directly).

## Decisions

- `Hold` is the shape: `Handle(Axis)` is the free move's held arrow, `Following { axis, at }` is the
  carried piece. One `Drag`, one distance path, two ways of holding it.
- The take is a **request** (`TranslateGizmo::grab`) and not a direct start: the click that names the
  piece is read by the shell after the gizmo has run for its frame, so the hold starts on the next
  one, from the click's own point. The piece is measured where the hand took it, and the first frame
  of the hold never jumps.
- The axis is latched at the first travel past `NAMING_TRAVEL = 4` points and kept. A piece that
  re-decided every frame would flip between two axes near 45° and its distance would restart from
  zero on each flip.
- The distance is still `closest_parameter_on_line`, so the carried piece and the arrow drag answer
  the same number for the same movement — the exact-distance fields, the capture paint and the rule
  need no change at all.
- The click that puts the piece down is checked **before** the axis latch: a piece taken and put back
  without travelling ends its hold even though no axis was ever named. That ordering was a real bug
  the test caught.
- Leaving the furniture move cancels the hold (`move_kind` is read again each frame); a click that
  opened a container is navigation and does not take a piece (`entered_level`).
- The band's hover and the status bar's hold line carry the words (`FurnitureHint`,
  `FurnitureHold`); the work cursor stays the one square of ADR 0121.

## Ruled out

- A 3-axis camera-plane move with the rule applied per axis: it reads well for a cabinet and it breaks
  the panel rule in the hand — the board would slide sideways by whatever the rule did not zero — and
  the exact-distance fields, the capture line and the typed number all describe *one* axis.
- A free-screen drag that picks the axis continuously (no latch): the distance restarts on every
  switch, so the piece jumps back to where it was taken each time the hand crosses 45°.
- Starting the hold on the press and dropping it on the release: one click would take and drop at
  once, so a plain click in the furniture move could not select anything.
- A hover-follow (no take click at all): the piece would move under a pointer that was only passing
  by, and there would be no gesture to cancel.
- A separate `furniture_gizmo.rs`: the preview, the distance, the rule, the fields and the capture are
  the same code, and N22 already ruled the copy out once.
