# X137 — the two ends of a row keep the keyboard through Enter, Tab and Escape

the two ends of a row keep the keyboard through Enter, Tab and Escape

## What was measured

- `DistanceFields::reference` (`crates/ui/src/distance.rs`) measured every field from the locked opposite face, for both layouts, so Enter on the far plate of an evenly fill's row turned the near plate from its air into the span across the row. A headless run over `evenly_harness` reproduced 80/120 reading as 600/120, while `model::fill_row` kept `plan.low = 80`: the boards never moved, the near field stopped showing its own number.
- The keyboard walk after Tab was not the overlay's. `egui`'s `Focus::begin_pass` latches `focus_direction` from the raw Tab event before any widget runs, and `Focus::end_pass` applies it to the frame after it; `InputState::consume_key` removes the event but not the latch. The walk therefore moved the focus off the plate `begin` had just asked for with `Scrub::show`'s `request_focus`, and `DistanceFields::end` stored whatever held the focus then: after a lock drew its glyph the run read `owns_the_keyboard == false` and a typed number landed nowhere. `Memory::move_focus(FocusDirection::None)` clears the latch; both it and `FocusDirection` are public in egui 0.37.
- `suspended` was cleared only by `reset` and `dismiss`, so Escape ended the keyboard for the rest of the gesture: `cancel()` sets `active` to `None` and `begin` refuses to auto-activate while suspended, so Tab had no side to walk from and no plate could take a number until the tool was disarmed and armed again.

## Decisions

- A row's `reference` is its own face, lock or no lock: the ends of a row are two airs, each measured from its own wall (X135). The rigid part's "measure from the locked face" rule stays with `Distances::new` and never reaches `row_ends`.
- The Tab key clears egui's latched direction instead of the overlay keeping a focus order of its own: a second tab order beside egui's would be one more thing to keep in step with the plates and the card.
- Tab and a pointer move both lift the Escape suspension; with no active side the first Tab puts the keyboard on the end nearest the pointer and the next Tab walks to the other. A still pointer keeps Escape's answer (no plate steals the keyboard back on its own frame), and a moving hand is the maker coming back to the gesture.

## Ruled out

- Clearing `suspended` unconditionally on the frame after Escape: Escape would be a no-op, because `begin` would auto-activate the plate and take the keyboard straight back.
- Walking on the first Tab after Escape (nearest, then the opposite in one press): it would hand the keyboard to the end the pointer does not name first.
- Fixing the Tab with plate ids: they were already stable (`Id::unique(("distance_plate", index))`) and the walk happened after the frame, not in the id.
