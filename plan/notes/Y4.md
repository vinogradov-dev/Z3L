# Y4 — the noise a pixel carries is measured, and an edge-avoiding filter spends it

At 128 samples a path tracer is grainy, and the reference project measured its à-trous filter as
taking about a third off that at 8 samples while recording where the filter stops paying and starts
smearing (Y1's measurement table). This task builds ours and measures it the same way: every number
below is printed by a test, not hoped for by the entry.

## What was measured

- `wc -l` after the work: `crates/final/src` 6 496 Rust + 1 128 WGSL, of which `denoise.rs` is 771
  lines with its tests and the shader's own share is ~45 (the variance, the guide and the retirement
  test). Nothing of the filter itself is in WGSL.
- **Three levels against five.** Five levels reach 32 pixels and *lowered* the picture on this fixture
  instead of denoising it: the boards are 400–600 mm wide, so a 32-pixel kernel spans a board and its
  lamp's falloff, and filtering a convex radiance field under a normalized kernel darkens it. Measured
  on the two-board fixture at 128 samples in a 64 × 48 image: the pale board reads 0.3479 converged,
  0.3338 after five levels (a 4 % darkening) and 0.3409 after three. On a 256 × 192 image, where the
  same three levels span a quarter of the same board, the error against the reference is 0.23 % of the
  picture's RMS against the raw image's 0.76 %.
- **The entry's first clause**, two boards at 256 × 192, 128 samples against a 4 096-sample reference:
  raw RMSE **0.001280**, denoised **0.000393** — 69 % of the error gone, on a picture whose RMS is
  0.167940. The join between the boards reads 0.31819 raw and 0.31829 filtered, so the error was not
  bought with the edge.
- **The entry's second clause**, 128 × 96 at 4 096 samples: the filter moves the picture by **0.238
  code values RMS**, a quarter of the one the entry allows — the blend's `r²/(r²+t²)` is near zero
  for a pixel whose own error bar is already under the threshold.
- **The entry's third clause**,
  `denoise::tests::a_two_millimetre_band_on_a_thousand_millimetre_panel_keeps_its_edge`: 1000 mm
  across 512 columns, a two-column dark band with a hundredth of noise either side; the band keeps
  over 90 % of its own contrast while the board beside it loses a third of its spread.
- **Retirement**: a deterministic frame (a white plate in a white room) at 256 samples leaves every
  pixel at exactly `MIN_SAMPLES` = 32 and the picture is still the room; the noisy fixture at 128
  leaves every pixel at 128, so retirement costs nothing where there is nothing to save.
- **The filter's cost on the host**: 1.23 megapixels of three levels in **243 ms** on sixteen cores
  (0.2 s per megapixel); the first version, single-threaded with an index computed per tap, took
  1.64 s. An A4 at 300 dpi therefore costs ~1.8 s of filtering against ~15 s of tracing
  (`docs/performance.md`).

## Decisions

- The filter is Rust on the host and the shader's part is only bookkeeping: it writes the sum, the sum
  of squares and the guide, and it retires a pixel. One implementation, measured by tests that need no
  device; no WGSL twin of the kernel to drift from it.
- The variance of the mean is the unbiased estimator over the *per-pixel* count, because retirement
  makes the count differ per pixel: `Image::from_accumulation` divides each pixel by its own count.
- The guide is albedo, facing normal and the primary ray's distance, one record of four `vec4`s: a
  compute shader has eight storage bindings and the frame's faces, nodes, two tables and two decor
  arrays take six of them, so accumulator, second moment and guide had to travel in one buffer.
- The distance stop predicts from the centre's depth gradient rather than tolerating a fixed step
  (Dammertz 2010, Schied 2017): on a floor at a glancing angle a neighbour sixteen pixels away is
  hundreds of millimetres deeper and is still the same floor. The gradient is the *smaller* of the two
  one-sided differences, so a real step is not "explained" by its own jump — with a central difference
  a 1 m step read as a slope and the filter crossed it.
- The stops are rational (`1/(1+x)`, `cos³²`) rather than exponential: thirty operations per tap is
  120 per pixel per level on the host, and the falloff's exact shape is not observable in a picture.
  The kernel is the B3 spline at a step of `2^level`.
- `TARGET_RELATIVE_ERROR` is 0.2 % — under a tenth of a code value of sRGB at a mid grey — and it is
  one number with two jobs: the blend's tolerance and the retirement test. The first value tried was
  2 %, and it made a 4096-sample request and a 32-sample one the same picture wherever a pixel
  converged, because retirement stopped both at the same place.

## Ruled out

- **The filter as a compute pass.** It would have been the crate's third transcription, and a strip
  needs a pad either way: the tracer now traces 8 rows beyond its own so the widest tap has a
  neighbour, which is the same work on either side of the boundary.
- **Five levels** (measured above: it darkens a board) and **one level** (0.43 %, a code value and a
  half on a mid grey).
- **A rough metal as the fixture.** Its highlight is real radiance that varies within the guide's own
  terms, so a filter of any width reads it as noise: the first fixture, two rough boards, moved the
  image *away* from the reference by 150 %. The fixture is two diffuse boards under three lamps, which
  is where noise in this tracer actually comes from — a lamp drawn by emitted power.
- **Albedo demodulation** (divide by the albedo, filter, multiply back): worth it where a texture
  varies and the noise does not, and our decors arrive as one albedo per pixel with no separate noise
  model. Recorded for the day a textured board at 64 samples needs it.
- **A temporal filter**: a final render is one image, not a sequence (ADR 0210).
- **Copying the reference's `denoise_atrous.wgsl`**: read once for the shape of the three stops, as
  Y1's discipline says, and every line here is written from the papers and measured by our own tests.

## What is left

- The blend does not know that a pixel straddles an edge: an antialiased silhouette pixel carries one
  surface's guide and two surfaces' light, and the filter treats it as its guide's surface. It shows as
  a one-pixel halo only where that pixel's error bar is above the threshold, and the cure is a guide
  that records coverage, not a wider kernel.
- The render place has no switch for the filter and no preview of the last image (Y6 owns the place's
  next increment). A person who wants the raw estimator has `Accumulation::raw_image` in the crate and
  no surface.
- A strip's pad rows are traced twice: 64 rows a strip at three levels, about an eighth of a strip. A
  filter on the device would not need them.
- The map's `Final render` row names `crates/final/src/denoise.rs` (`LEVELS`,
  `TARGET_RELATIVE_ERROR`, `Guide`, `Sample`, `Frame::denoise`, `variance_of_mean`, `retires`,
  `blend_weight`), `GpuPixel` and `pad_rows` when the integration session closes Y4: a stream leaves
  `plan/map.md` to the primary.
