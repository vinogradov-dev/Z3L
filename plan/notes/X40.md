# X40 — Why U88 landed poorly

Why U88 landed poorly

## What the owner saw

The square is not the window's pointer. It is the fallback for "the scene has no gesture", so a
contour tool shows the system arrow and an armed primitive the system crosshair. It is painted inside
the viewport on the scene layer, so the floating cluster covers it and the product card leaves no
pointer at all. Its one amber ink vanishes on a light board.

## Root causes, in the order they cost the most

1. **The task modelled a fallback, not a pointer.** The entry said "a gesture that owns its own cursor
   keeps the OS icon and the square is not painted". That sentence is the bug: it makes every tool a
   competing owner of the pointer, so the square can only appear where no tool and no gesture exists.
   The owner's sentence — the square is the default for everything except a menu — was never the
   contract; the contract was U83's amber ink and a fallback.
2. **The cursor was owned by the wrong layer.** `Document::viewport` cannot know what the shell draws
   after it. `Chrome` covers the ribbon, the side panels and the method list, but not the floating
   cluster, the status bar, the product card or a modal. The square was therefore clipped to
   `chrome.content(area)` and painted on the central panel's background layer, under every
   `Order::Middle` area and every `Order::Foreground` modal. The "one cursor decision" was one place
   inside one function, not one place in the window.
3. **`CursorIcon::None` was treated as "the square is up".** A modal then drew over the hidden system
   arrow: the square was below it and the system arrow was suppressed, so the pointer disappeared.
   The model had no state for "a surface above the pointer wants the system arrow".
4. **Contrast was not an acceptance criterion.** The task inherited one ink from ADR 0111, which was
   written for guides *over the model*, not for a pointer that must be found on a white board, a dark
   board and translucent chrome. A single warm amber on a light scene is not a pointer; it is a
   tint. "Visible" was not in `Done:`.
5. **The tests measured the implementation, not the experience.** They asserted `cursor == None`,
   the square's centre and its width. Every one of those passes while the square is painted under the
   cluster or under a modal. No test put the pointer over the bottom cluster, over an opened product
   card, or over an armed contour tool. The list-cursor test even encoded the wrong contract
   (`Crosshair` for an armed tool).
6. **The eye check could not run.** The offscreen shot was `#[ignore]`d because it needs a GPU, and it
   photographed the scene alone: no cluster, no modal, no toolbar. The one test that could have seen
   the occlusion was both optional and pointed at the wrong rectangle.
7. **No live pass.** The task was closed from a green gate and a headless frame. A single minute of
   moving the pointer over the bottom toolbar and the product card would have produced the owner's
   whole report.

## What this task changes

The square becomes a window-level overlay on `Order::Tooltip`: the frame resets the system cursor,
draws every surface, and only then hides the arrow and paints the square. Menus, popups and modals
are `Order::Foreground`; there the system arrow stays and no square is drawn. A widget that sets a
cursor of its own (text, divider, scrub) keeps it. The viewport's `owned_cursor` chain and every
tool's `cursor()` go. The outline is a fixed 3 pt dark + 1 pt light pair, so one of the two strokes
reads on any surface; the crosshair keeps the same pair and clips to the scene rectangle. The new
ADR records the pointer as chrome, not as a guide, so ADR 0111 keeps the amber for model guides.

The verification changes with the contract: every test names the surface under the pointer — scene,
panel, cluster, list, product card, text field, divider — and the shot moves the pointer onto the
bottom cluster so the picture can prove the square is above it.
