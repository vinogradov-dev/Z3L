# X87 — the invisible floor is a bound of the drag, and its own kind of stop

## What was measured

- The floor already existed as a *snap*: `SnapSettings::planes` captures a moved face onto Z = 0 within
  the workspace's 5 mm tolerance (`snap_distance` in `crates/model/src/project/move_query.rs`), and
  `SnapSurface::Plane` is the capture it reports. It only fires when the snapping is on and the face
  comes near; nothing refused a place under the floor.
- The only height rule was the mount trait: `axes_of` takes Z away from a `Floor` class, so a base
  cabinet could not be carried down and every other piece could (`the_mount_trait_decides_whether_a_product_keeps_its_height`).
- `MoveLimit::contact: Option<Id>` carried a node id, so a stop that no node owns had no way to say
  itself; `MoveRule::free_at` and `MoveRule::blocked_at` were two copies of the same predicate, one
  answering a bool and one a name.
- `Project::contact_rule` and `Project::move_rule` were two entry points differing in two facts at
  once — `ALL_MOVE_AXES` versus the document's axes, and `None` versus `Some(snaps)` — so a third
  fact (the floor) had nowhere to go that was not a lie in one of the two names.
- The settings value is `ui::settings::SnapSettings`, persisted with `#[serde(default)]` at the
  container, so a new field is `true` for a file written before it; `app/src/settings_store.rs`'s
  round-trip test asserts the section field by field.

## Decisions

- The floor is a **bound on the vertical travel**, computed once per drag from the moved boxes' own
  `min.z` (`floor_bound`): `delta.z >= min(0, -min.z)`. A body standing on the floor may not sink at
  all, one standing above it may descend that far, and one the document already holds below it may
  only be lifted — that last clamp is what stops a typed coordinate from becoming a trap.
- The stop is its own type: `Stop::Node { id, kind, min, max }` or `Stop::Floor`. A capture says
  where a piece jumped, a stop says what refused it; `MoveLimit::contact` and `MoveRule::blocked_at`
  both answer `Stop`, and the shell's log writes `Some(Floor)` through `stop_text`.
- One settings value per drag: `MoveSettings { axes: MoveAxes, snaps: Option<SnapSettings>, floor }`,
  with `MoveSettings::free()` as the free tool's reading. `Project::contact_rule` is gone; the free
  move asks `move_rule` with all three axes, nothing to snap to, and the same floor.
- `MoveRule::free_at` is `blocked_at` without a name, so the destination-first branch of ADR 0166
  cannot take a place the axis walk would refuse, and the log asks the drag's own question.
- The floor cuts the axis exactly as `stop_at_contact` does — the per-axis cut is clamped before the
  candidate is tested — so a diagonal carry stops on Z and keeps travelling on X and Y, and the
  retry pass of ADR 0171 still runs.

## Ruled out

- **A virtual obstacle box under Z = 0.** It would need an `Id` for a surface no node owns, or a
  `Stop` on `SurfaceBox` that every other reader of that type (`niche`, `probe`, `fill`) would then
  have to handle. The bound is one comparison on one axis and touches nothing else.
- **A new `SurfaceKind::Floor`.** `SurfaceKind` describes what owns a *document* surface; the floor is
  the workspace's, and `void.rs` and `measure_gizmo.rs` read that enum as document truth.
- **Putting the switch inside `SnapSettings` and keeping the two entry points.** The free move needs
  the floor without the snaps, so either `contact_rule` grew a second argument or `SnapSettings` stopped
  meaning what its name says. One `MoveSettings` per drag answers both.
- **Blocking the write as well as the drag** (a `Resting`-style guard on Z = 0). The floor is a
  maker's help and not a document invariant: a scene written by an older build, a typed number or a
  future import may place a part below it, and the document must still open and be repairable.
- **Pushing a below-floor body up on the first drag.** It would refuse a sideways carry for a reason
  the maker did not ask about, and the minimum of a drag is not a repair.
