# X85 — the contact port, and what it costs to answer with a body's own surface

## What was measured

- `parry3d` 0.31 is an **f32-only** crate: the `[features]` table has `f32` and no `f64`, although the
  source still carries `#[cfg(feature = "f64")]` blocks. The double build is a separate crate,
  `parry3d-f64` 0.31 (Apache-2.0, `build = false`, glam-based through `glamx` 0.3), and that is the one
  this project uses: the document stores micrometres and a workshop is metres wide, which f32 cannot
  resolve.
- `glamx` 0.3 pins `glam` 0.33 while the workspace is on 0.34, so the tree carries two `glam` builds.
  parry's `Vec3` and our `DVec3` are therefore *different types with the same fields*: the adapter
  converts components (and a quaternion through `Rot3::from_xyzw`), which is exact and costs four
  numbers per pose. Downgrading the workspace to `glam` 0.33 was the alternative; the duplicate build
  is the cheaper price and the only consumer of the old one is the adapter.
- The layer gate refuses a dependency on a crate **of the same layer**, so a `contact` port crate at L0
  could not depend on `geom` (also L0) — and the port cannot do without vectors. `model` (L1) needs the
  port, so the port cannot sit at L1. Hence the port is a **module of `geom`** (`geom::contact`), and
  only the adapter is a crate (L2). This is the packaging the owner's choice of "port + injected
  adapter" survives the layer map with; a separate port crate would need a same-layer edge.
- `parry3d-f64` 0.31.1 resolves (0.31.0 is the version the entry named), pulls `spade`, `rstar` only
  through `alloc`, and has no build script: `cargo xtask layers`' C-vendoring check passes on the whole
  tree.
- parry's answers are in the shapes' own millimetres (`Real = f64` in the `-f64` build), so the adapter
  converts nothing but components: a distance out of parry is a Z3L millimetre.
- `cast_shapes` takes a velocity vector and returns a time of impact in the velocity's own units, so the
  adapter normalises the heading and the answer is a distance in millimetres whatever the caller passed.
  `ShapeCastOptions { stop_at_penetration: true }` makes a pair that already touches answer zero, which
  is what a drag standing on a face needs.

## Decisions

- Port: `geom::contact` with `Shape::{Convex, Mesh}`, `Pose`, `Penetration` and **two** traits — the
  adapter's `Contact::prepare(shape, pose) -> Option<Shared>` and the body's `Solid::{bounds, shifted,
  travel, penetration}`, with `overlap(a, b, slack)` as a free function. The first draft had `travel`
  and `penetration` on `Contact` taking both `Prepared` handles and both poses; that forces `&dyn
  Contact` into `MoveRule` and so a lifetime onto a type the shell keeps across frames. Preparing a
  body that carries its own pose removes the lifetime, keeps the conversion once per gesture, and a
  body of another adapter answers `None` instead of being measured wrongly (trait upcasting to `Any`
  does the downcast).
- Adapter: `crates/contact-parry`, L2, composition-only, the only crate allowed to depend on `parry`.
  `cargo xtask layers` carries the rule beside the `truck` one.
- Only three questions for now. A gap distance and a broad phase are the entry's stage B; an unused
  query is a promise the code cannot keep, and the frame budget is measured before a cache is designed.
- A shape the adapter cannot build answers `None` from `prepare`, so the document falls back to the
  world-box reading it has today rather than refusing a file. That fallback is what the next step has to
  keep honest: the rule must never mix the two readings for one pair.
- The tests measure the three cases the entry names: a box pair's sunk depth, a cast that stops at the
  first touch, a turned board measured by its own edge (385.86 mm where the box reading says 372.22), and
  a ball that falls through a square hole in a plate but rubs the rim 30 mm off centre.

## Ruled out

- **`parry3d` 0.31 (f32).** A workshop is 10 m wide and the document stores micrometres: the ulp of f32
  at 10 m is about 1 µm, and every subtraction of two world coordinates loses that again.
- **Scaling millimetres to metres to help f32.** Relative precision does not change with the unit —
  f32's ~7 digits are the limit, not the magnitude.
- **A separate `contact` crate at L0 or L1.** L0 cannot reach `geom`; L1 is where `model` sits. See the
  measurement above.
- **Binding parry inside the port crate** (the other road in `plan/questions.md`): the owner chose the
  injected adapter, and it keeps parry out of `geom` and lets a stub answer contact in a test.
- **Downgrading the workspace's `glam` to 0.33** to remove the second build: it changes a foundation
  crate for one adapter's pin, and the conversion is four numbers at a boundary that already exists.

## What landed, in order

1. `7af9f8b` the port in `geom::contact` and the `parry3d-f64` adapter, with four adapter tests.
2. `0847405` the port's second shape: a prepared body carries its own pose, so no `&dyn Contact` has to
   travel through `MoveRule`.
3. the shape builder (`project::contact_shape`) and the seam: `move_rule` and `resting_conflict` take
   `Option<&dyn Contact>`, `Mover`/`Obstacle`/`Measured` carry the prepared body, and `blocked_at`,
   `stop_at_contact` and `resting_inside` ask it when both sides have one. Two model tests prove the
   reading: a 100 mm ball dragged past a corner travels to 211 mm where its box stops at 200, and the
   guard grants the same place the drag showed.

The engine's `refuse_resting` still passes `None`: a command has no adapter, and the drag must not be
flipped alone (a preview on surfaces with a release on boxes refuses the place it showed, ADR 0166).
Flipping both means the adapter reaches a session and every command that writes a placement.

## What is left

1. `model` builds a `Shape` per node from its own parametric data — cone, cylinder, ball, box and panel
   are convex hulls; a notched panel and a compound are triangle surfaces — and caches it by revision.
2. `MoveRule`, `resting_inside` and the probe ask the port instead of `SurfaceBox`, with one log and one
   refusal sentence; the three Done cases of the entry become model tests.
3. Stage B: a broad phase and a per-revision shape cache, then the frame budget of a big scene.
