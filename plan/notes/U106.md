# U106 — the corner display is a diagnostic block, measured where the work happens

## What was measured

- The switch *Show projection and angles* (`crates/text/src/messages.rs`, `ShowProjectionAngles`)
  drew one line: `gizmo::paint_readout` under the cube, all of it inside `Gizmo::show`, so the
  numbers disappeared with the cube's own switch (`ViewCluster::show_widget`) and nothing else in the
  program printed a frame rate, a frame time or a scene cost.
- The two defects the owner's 5 000-sphere scene hits were measured by earlier sessions and filed:
  X58 draws every drawable whatever the camera sees (0.48 M / 1.9 M / 4.8 M triangles at 500 / 2 000
  / 5 000 spheres, 9.5 ms of an empty frame at the top count), X59 ray-casts every triangle of the
  scene per click (16 / 64 / 161 ms at the same counts).
- An upload is cheap even at that size, so a drag's per-frame upload is not the delay:
  `render::cache::FaceKey` and `EdgeKey` identify a mesh by the address of its `Arc` and compare a
  transform and a colour, so one upload of a 5 000-drawable frame is 5 000 hash lookups and one
  `Vec`, and a preview that moves one drawable rebuilds exactly that one.
- `Document::pick_scene` had three call sites — a click, a right click and the press that arms the
  rubber band — and none was timed; `Document::refresh` already timed the rebuild into `Rebuild` and
  `upload_scene` timed nothing.
- `egui` reports the interval between frames as `InputState::stable_dt`, which is the one number a
  frame rate may be derived from without a clock of our own.

## Decisions

- One meter on the document (`app/src/osd.rs`, `Meter`) holds the smoothed frame time and the last
  rebuild, upload and pick; the code that spends a duration records it, and the block reads the
  meter on the next frame. Nothing is estimated and nothing is measured twice.
- The frame rate is an exponential average with a weight of one quarter on the newest frame: a hitch
  moves it, a single outlier cannot swing it to zero, and a scene that becomes heavy is visible
  within a few frames.
- The block is its own module and its own switch, and it outlives the cube: the cube is a view
  control, the block is a diagnostic readout, and one switch must not take the other with it. The
  cube's rectangle stays the anchor, so the block keeps the placement rule that already clears the
  panels and the ribbon band (U73).
- While the block is on, `Document::viewport` asks for a repaint every frame: a rate measured on a
  window that repaints only on input is the idle rate. Off by default, so nobody pays for the frames.
- An unmeasured number is not printed: no `fps` line before the first frame, no `pick` line before
  the first pick. The scene cost is always printed, because an empty frame is a measurement too.
- The pick timer sits on the three whole-scene pick paths through `Document::pick_scene` and
  `Document::pick_surface`, so the number describes the scene rather than one call site; the band's
  press goes through `Document::over_empty_space`, which times the two picks it asks.
- The block is recorded in ADR 0140; the words are five new rows in `crates/text` and the camera line
  keeps `OrientationReadout`.

## Ruled out

- Folding the rate into the status bar: the bar is a document and selection readout that every
  workspace draws, and a number that changes every frame there would flicker beside the snap and
  the projection chips.
- A capability row in `crates/ui/src/inventory.rs`: the block dispatches no command, writes nothing
  and has no key, so it is a view switch beside the cube and the contact shadows, not a capability
  (ADR 0117).
- A real profiler surface (a frame graph, a timeline, GPU timestamps): a second host and a second
  design, for a question this block answers. Revisit when the block stops being enough.
- Keeping the line in `gizmo.rs` beside the cube: the cube would then own the rate, the scene cost
  and the pick of a viewport it knows nothing about, and its switch would keep hiding them.
- Measuring the pick with a sampling timer or a counter instead of `Instant`: a duration is what the
  question asks, and one `Instant` pair per pick costs nothing beside the pick.
