# X102 — a primitive is a body, so a boolean cuts it in both directions

## What was measured

- `crates/param/src/constructive.rs:71` — a boolean step's tool was a `Constructive`, and `compile`
  had one way to start a body: a ring swept on Z. A primitive has neither a ring nor a height, so the
  compile refused it, and `BooleanTool.body: Constructive` could not even store one.
- `crates/ui/src/toolbars/ready.rs:252` — the three Model cells enabled on `selected_body`
  (constructive only) and answered `BooleanNeedsBody` on a primitive: the owner's gesture was
  explained and still not a cut.
- **Road (a) works.** A closed triangle soup — one planar face per triangle, vertices and edges
  shared by index so every edge is used twice with opposite winding — is `geometric_consistent`,
  `ShellCondition::Closed`, and `truck_shapeops` booleans it. A 32 x 16 sphere (960 triangles,
  r = 10 mm) builds in 4 ms and cuts a board in 12 ms; 3 968 triangles cost 100 ms and 16 128 cost
  613 ms, which is where `MAX_MESH_BODY_TRIANGLES` refuses with a sentence.
- **The kernel cannot divide a face that holds a ring of the other body's vertices.** A ball drawn
  around its centre stands on the face it was drawn on, and a uv sphere with an equator ring has a
  whole ring of vertices in that plane: "This wire is not simple". The same ball with no ring at the
  equator cuts to 177 939 mm3 against 177 906 analytically.
- **A solid of revolution from exact surfaces is not an alternative.** `builder::cone` +
  `rsweep` builds a closed ball of the right volume (33 338 mm3 against 33 510), and *every* boolean
  against it answers "produced no solid": `truck`'s shape support does not cover the surface type a
  revolution produces.
- A mesh body as a **target** is accurate: for a 16 mm prism through a 20 mm ball, difference +
  intersection summed to the ball's own volume across 12 configurations (tessellations and a turned
  block), and the notch matched the numeric integral of the chord over the prism within 0.5%.

## Decisions

- **Road (a): the port learns `MeshBody`.** A validated closed, consistently wound triangle surface
  in millimetres, built by `SolidKernel::mesh`. It is the one shape no extrusion describes, and the
  one an imported part will need.
- **`Primitive` carries its steps.** A `PrimitiveShape` plus `steps: Vec<Boolean>`: a step needs a
  home on the body it built, not a reason of its own, and a primitive had none. The owner's question
  — why a boolean needs a history — is answered by the arrangement, not by a new rule.
- **`BooleanTool.body` is a `ToolBody`:** a drawn history or a primitive's parameters, tagged so a
  file needs no node and no library to rebuild the result (ADR 0197 keeps).
- **The mesh builder moves from `mesh` (L3) to `param` (L2).** The compile runs in `param` and a
  crate cannot call one above it; `mesh` already called `param`, so the direction and the one owner
  of the evaluator both keep.
- **Two tessellations of a sphere.** The drawn mesh keeps its rings and its full pole fan (X55). The
  body the kernel divides stands its rings half a step off the equator, because "a ring of vertices
  in the face" is exactly what the kernel cannot resolve. Measured cost of the alternative (offset
  rings everywhere): the pole fan thins from 512 edges to 136 at 256 sides.
- **A mesh cutter grows one micrometre along its own normals**, the same trade the drawn cutter's
  overshoot makes (ADR 0197).
- **`compile_tool` composes frames.** A nested tool's placement is expressed in its parent's frame,
  so a tool that carries steps of its own now lands where the picture says; the old code read the
  placement in the frame the whole history was built in. One existing test was asserting the buggy
  position — its numbers are now the composed frame's (129 600 mm3, not 135 000).

## Ruled out

- **Road (b): a revolve in `SolidPlan`.** It would add a second base kind to the plan for two shapes
  and still leave a mesh unusable as a cutter; and the adapter measurement above shows a revolved
  solid is not an operand at all in this kernel.
- **A primitive target that refuses in words.** The first reading of the task (a primitive is a tool
  only, a primitive target is refused) would have left the owner's own gesture — select the sphere,
  press Subtract — uncut. The model now lets a primitive be a target, so the question dissolved.
- **A separate `Geometry` variant for a primitive with steps.** It would have moved the node's payload
  kind on the first cut and taken `as_primitive` away from every snap, contact and card path.
- **Making a primitive an exact sphere/cone B-rep in `truck`**: no boolean support for the surfaces a
  revolution produces, measured above.
