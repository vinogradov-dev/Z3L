# U120 — a round body answers its own places, not the corners of its box

## What was measured

- `Scene::object` filed `object_bounds_mm`'s box for every geometry and one extra centre for a
  primitive. A cylinder — a drawn circle swept on Z — therefore answered the eight corners of the
  square around its base (points in the air), the box's edge middles, six box face centres and one
  middle; its upper rim and its axis ends were nowhere.
- A cone answered its box and its middle, never its apex or its base circle.
- A ball's six `Centre` marks were already on its surface at its radius, because the box's face
  centres are transformed by the node's own placement; the same box also filed 8 corners and 12 edge
  middles in the air around it.
- `testkit::sample_project` is two panels and a container, so the frame tests that ask `snap_marks`
  never see a round body; the one owner-facing test of an object body is the drawn cube and the ball
  in `a_body_and_a_ball_answer_a_snap_on_every_corner_edge_face_and_centre`.
- That cube answers the middle of a side face and of its four vertical edges only through the box,
  which is the fix U116 landed for the owner's report ("a drawn cube snapped only to the profile it
  was drawn from").

## Decisions

- **A payload files its own places.** `Scene::object` matches the geometry, and each arm answers with
  what its parameters name. The box is filed only where it *is* the body: a baked mesh — the one
  payload with no parameters left — and a body swept from an axis-aligned rectangle.
- **The rectangle test is structural and cheap** (`is_axis_rectangle`): four straight sides that run
  along the ring's own axes close a rectangle and nothing else. It needs no `PathShape` tag, so a cube
  drawn by four clicks keeps the box; a tilted rectangle, a hexagon or a ring with a cut corner does
  not, because its box corners stand in the air and its rims and axis ends are what the body has.
  Keeping the box for a drawn cube is what keeps U116's guarantee and its frame test.
- **A round body files its two rims and its axis ends.** A swept circle files the ring it was drawn
  from and the same ring lifted by the extrusion height (`ring`/`segments` take a `lift` in
  millimetres), plus the middle of each end. A circle's arc centres already put a middle at each rim;
  the explicit axis ends are what a polygon profile, which has no arc centres, needs.
- **A primitive's places come from its parameters**, and the match over `Primitive` has no catch-all:
  a new primitive is a compile error until it answers (`Scene::primitive`).
- **A rim's quadrants are `Vertex` and a body's middle is `Centre`.** A drawn circle already files its
  arc ends and their middles, so a ball's poles, a cone's rims and a swept circle's rims read alike;
  a ball's middle stays `Centre` because ADR 0139 keeps it a place the maker names inside the
  material.
- **A ball's six extremes now follow the radius instead of the box.** They are the same six points,
  but a test turns the ball a quarter turn about X and finds its pole on the world Y, so the marks are
  the body's own and no longer a by-product of a box.

## Ruled out

- **Taking "the bounding box leaves the snap scene" literally for every payload.** It would strip a
  drawn cube of the middle of its side faces and of its four vertical edges — the answer U116 landed
  after the owner's report — and rewrite
  `a_body_and_a_ball_answer_a_snap_on_every_corner_edge_face_and_centre` down to the two rims. The
  box that an axis-aligned rectangular sweep fills *is* what its parameters describe, so it stays.
- **Classifying a body with `contact_shape::shape_beyond_box`** (the X92 reading, `describes_box`): it
  builds the body's own shape, and the snap scene is rebuilt on every pointer move. The rectangle
  test asks the same question of the profile in four segments.
- **A `Primitive::places` API in `primitive.rs`.** The places are snap candidates and every other
  payload is filed in the same `Scene`, so the match lives beside `boxed`/`ring`/`rim` instead of
  exporting a second vocabulary nothing else reads. The task's `files:` hint named `primitive.rs` and
  `tree.rs`; neither needed a change.
- **A flat chain's box.** A Path's box adds corners in the air for a diagonal chain and duplicates the
  ends of an axis-aligned one, so a path and a contour set answer their own segments only.
- **Giving a ball an equator arc.** The task names the quadrants, not the circle, and an Edge kind
  along a ball's equator would be a silhouette the maker cannot tell from the sphere's outline.

## What the tests pin

- A cylinder: a rim quadrant, an axis end, nothing at the corner of the square (kind `None` with the
  grid off), and every mark at the body's radius or on its axis.
- A cone: the apex, the base circle as an `Edge`, a truncated cone's top rim and top middle, and every
  mark inside the cone's own radii and height.
- A ball: 7 marks, each on its middle or at its radius, with a turned ball's pole on the world Y.
- A swept rectangle: 8 corners, 12 edge middles and 6 face centres, the middle of a side face
  included — U116's guarantee, pinned at the model level.
- A flat diagonal chain: three marks and no box corner.
- The headless frame: a swept circle and a cone captured at a rim, at an axis end and at the apex, and
  the air beside the square's corner capturing nothing of either.
