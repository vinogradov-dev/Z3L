# X86 — the depth a pair is sunk to, not the volume it shares

## What was measured

- `resting_inside` (`crates/model/src/project/move_query.rs`) exempted *any* pair the document already
  held overlapping: `was.is_some_and(|was| was.meets(obstacle))` answered "no conflict" for every
  destination, and `MoveRule::blocked_at` carried the same exemption behind a `!nested(start)` guard.
  So a part left inside another by a hand edit or a typed coordinate could be pushed further in.
- `SurfaceBox::meets` is the exact separating-axis test of the two boxes' own frames; `shared` answers
  the three shared world extents. Neither answers "how deep".
- The sample scene already holds a coplanar pair (its shelf and the drawer's front share a box), and
  `crates/ui/src/placement_card.rs::a_translation_edit_keeps_the_exact_quaternion` sets a rotated
  placement on that shelf — a rule that refuses an already overlapping pair a *turn* breaks a document
  the editor ships.

## Decisions

- The measure is the **sunk depth**: `SurfaceBox::depth` is the least overlap over `separating_axes`, a
  length in millimetres. A body pressed further into another needs a longer travel to be pushed apart,
  so the measure grows exactly while a piece sinks; a board slid along the face it lies in keeps the
  depth it had, because its thinnest overlap is its own thickness whichever way it slides; a pair that
  only touches answers zero.
- The exemption changes from "any destination" to "no deeper than the place it stands", with
  `CONTACT_MM` (1 µm) as the slack: `now.depth(obstacle) > was.depth(obstacle) + CONTACT_MM` is the
  refusal. The drag asks the same comparison in `blocked_at`, and `resting_inside` keeps its own gate
  (`was.meets`), so the write and the drag agree on the destination.
- A deeper destination is refused, never trimmed: the axis keeps the piece where it stands and
  `blocked_at` names the part. The depth a pair is sunk to is not a number the maker picked, so there is
  no place to stop at between "here" and "the hand's place".
- A legitimate nesting is not that pair: the destination stays exempt while it is inside the holder, so
  a unit inside a niche keeps the wall cut of ADR 0102 rather than escaping through the depth answer.
  `nested(start)` therefore stays out of the drag's exemption, as it was.

## Ruled out

- **The shared volume** (`shared.x * shared.y * shared.z`). It was implemented first and failed two
  cases: a turn of an already overlapping part grows the world box's overlap without sinking anything
  (the placement-card test above), and two coplanar boards *lose* volume when one slides off the other,
  which is a slide and not a repair.
- **The penetration depth of the world boxes only** (`min(shared)`). It answers the same number as the
  exact test for a box pair, but it cannot see a turned body's own faces, and the pair's frames are what
  `meets` already uses.
- **A pop-out at the take** (the owner's other option in `plan/questions.md`): refused by the owner.
  It would need a preview placement nobody asked for, its own cancel and an undo entry that mixes a
  gesture with a computed move. The walk-out shape is the `Done` clause of the entry.
- **Trimming the drag to "as deep as before"**: the piece would creep into the other one while the hand
  pulls, which reads as the bug the entry reports.
