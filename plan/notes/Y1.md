## Decisions

- Our own tracer in `crates/final` (L5), `wgpu` + WGSL, borrowing the shell's device: the reference
  would add a third `wgpu` pin beside `render` and `egui-wgpu`, and those two must bump together.
- Its technique is taken as approach and its measurements as a starting hypothesis; its text is
  not. Copyright covers the expression — the file, its comments, its structure, its tests and its
  golden images — and not the algorithms, which are published. A translation or a "port with renamed
  variables" is still a derivative work, so the discipline is: read it once to understand what the
  pieces are, close it, write the algorithm down in our own words, and implement from the paper. With
  that discipline no notice and no `ATTRIBUTION` row is owed, and none is written.
- What is never borrowed even as an idea: its scene model, its material vocabulary, its API shape and
  its OBJ loader. Our input is the kernel's triangles, the catalogue's records and the document's
  lights.
- A binned-SAH BVH2 of our own instead of a compressed wide BVH: a whole kitchen is tens of thousands
  of triangles, and wide BVHs pay in the millions.
- The sampler and the BSDF maths live in pure Rust beside the shader, which is their transcription,
  so a wrong MIS weight fails a test on a machine with no GPU (§4.7).
- Correctness is checked against theory — the white furnace, the analytic radiance of a quad under a
  cosine lobe — and against our own converged reference, never against their images.
- Lights are the document's own objects in watts with inverse-square falloff (ADR 0039), not a
  colour-and-intensity pair.
- No bloom, no saturation, no HDRI, no EXR: the owner's answer on all four was "not important"; a
  catalogue image is graded in the print pipeline.

## Where the technique comes from

Each of these enters the ADR and the code comments as the citation, so the next reader argues with a
paper instead of with a file:

- the integrator, MIS and light selection — Veach, *Robust Monte Carlo Methods for Light Transport
  Simulation* (1997); Pharr, Jakob and Humphreys, *Physically Based Rendering*, 4th ed.;
- GGX and its visible-normal sampling — Walter et al. 2007; Heitz 2018, *Sampling the GGX
  Distribution of Visible Normals*;
- multiple-scattering energy compensation for rough conductors — Turquin 2019;
- Sobol with Owen scrambling — Sobol 1967; Burley 2020, *Practical Hash-based Owen Scrambling*;
- edge-avoiding à-trous filtering — Dammertz et al. 2010; the variance-guided blend — Schied et al.
  2017 (SVGF);
- tone mapping — Narkowicz 2015 (ACES fit), Reinhard 2002, the Khronos PBR Neutral specification;
- binned-SAH construction — Wald 2007.

## Ruled out

- `solstrale` as a dependency: measured above, and only about 40% of those lines are what a
  furniture scene needs; the `wgpu` pin and the tuning would follow a project with one maintainer.
- Copying anything out of it: Apache-2.0 would permit it with the notices kept, and §4.6 forbids it
  regardless, so the notices are not needed because the text is not taken.
- Cycles standalone: Apache-2.0, but C++ with OpenImageDenoise, Embree and OSL — §2 and §4.8.
  EEVEE is part of Blender and GPL-2.0+, which §2 stops on.
- `bevy_solari`: needs `bevy_render`, `bevy_pbr` and `bevy_ecs` — a second engine beside
  `crates/render`.
- `kajiya`: an application rather than a library, needs hardware ray tracing, last push 2025-07.
- `akari_render`: GPL-3.0. `rs_pbrt`, `rpt`, `rust-pathtracer`: educational or unmaintained since
  2021–2024, and `rpt` carries a network client.
- Hardware ray tracing as a requirement: `wgpu` 30 exposes `RAY_QUERY`, but a laptop without it must
  still produce the catalogue image, so traversal starts in compute.
- Photon mapping, spectral rendering and participating media: none of them is visible in furniture at
  the sample counts this feature can afford.
