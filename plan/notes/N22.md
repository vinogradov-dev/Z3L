# N22 — the furniture move is a tool of its own, and a panel slides along its thickness

## What was measured

- `app/src/main.rs` held three modes and one `transform()` that mapped a mode to a gesture; the
  viewport asked it in five places (`document.rs` update, paint, drag flag, consumed click, and the
  distance fields), so one new state needed no new branch anywhere else.
- `TranslateGizmo::update` read `Project::move_rule` unconditionally, after a `move_targets` of the
  selection: the rule was applied whenever the selection happened to be furniture, which is the
  coupling the task removes. The handle spec was the const `ALL_AXES`, and `hit_axis` and `paint`
  already took a `HandleSpec { axes, length }`, so gating the handles needed no new hit test.
- `MoveRule` stored `floor_bound: bool` and derived `allows` from it. That one field was the only
  user of the mount trait inside the rule, so a table of allowed axes replaced it without a second
  measurement.
- `MoveRule::limit` zeroed the vertical distance for a floor-bound product *before* snapping, so the
  same place refuses any axis the drag does not offer: no new branch in `snap_distance` or
  `blocking`.
- The panel's own frame puts its thickness on local Z (`docs/domain/products.md`), and
  `Placement::transform_vector` turns that normal by the whole parent chain, so
  `world_placement(panel)?.transform_vector(DVec3::Z)` is the panel's world thickness with no new
  query.
- `top_most_nodes` already drops a descendant whose ancestor is selected, so a cabinet and its own
  shelf never reach the axis intersection as two different answers.
- `status_bar.rs`'s `transform_mode_ui` was a loop over three `(mode, message)` pairs, and
  `frame_tests::mode_cell` finds a cell by its caption, so a fourth cell is one row of data.
- The exact-distance fields do not read the rule at all: `move_pinned` returns the pinned coordinate
  and `update` takes `Some(pinned)` before the pointer path, so a typed gap lands where typed in
  both modes and no rule can move it.

## Decisions

- `Project::furniture_axes(&[Id]) -> [bool; 3]` is the one answer, and `MoveRule` carries it, so the
  drawn handles and the rule's `allows` cannot disagree.
- The panel axis is the world axis the board's normal points most nearly at, because every surface
  the rule measures is a world box (ADR 0102); a board turned off the axes travels in one of the
  three world directions rather than along its exact normal.
- A selection offers the intersection of its nodes' axes. Two boards with different thickness axes
  therefore draw no handle, which is the honest rigid answer and not a refusal behind an armed
  handle (ADR 0113).
- The panel axis belongs to the tool, not to the document: a board at the root offers only its
  thickness even though `move_rule` answers `None` there and the drag keeps the exact distance.
- The free move keeps everything else it had — the pivot, the preview, both distance fields and one
  `node.placement.set_many` on release — and loses only the rule and the snapping.
- `MoveKind { Free, Furniture }` travels in `gizmo_math::Target` beside `snaps`: `TranslateGizmo::update`
  was already at clippy's seven arguments, and the target is the per-frame snapshot a drag reads
  (the same reason N21 put `SnapSettings` there).
- Two moves in the status bar band would read as the same word, so each of the four cells carries a
  hover line: the pair is one decision made two ways (ADR 0147).

## Ruled out

- A general world direction for the panel handle, along its exact normal: `MoveAxis`, `SurfaceBox`,
  `blocking` and `snap_distance` all measure an axis-aligned world box, so an arbitrary direction
  would need a second measurement of every surface.
- A separate `furniture_gizmo.rs`: the preview, the commit, the pinned distance and the fields are
  the same code, and copying them would make a second drag path to keep in step.
- Showing the two in-plane handles and refusing the drag: ADR 0113 already decided a half-live gizmo
  is the worst of the three.
- Reporting the old `move_rule` for a non-furniture selection so the free move kept its snapping: the
  owner asked for the snapping to turn off in the free move.
- A keyboard cell for the fourth mode here: `U113` owns the mode keys and owes this one its key.
- A headless test of the typed gap inside a furniture drag: the field needs focus and text events
  mid-drag; `distance.rs` tests the pin and `update` takes it before the rule, which is one branch.
