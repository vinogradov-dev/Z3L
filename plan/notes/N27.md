# N27 — the object move, and a solid body measured by its faces

the object move, and a solid body measured by its faces

## What was measured

- The rule already held **two** readings of one contact. `SurfaceBox::meets` is the exact
  separating-axis test over both boxes' own frames and the write's guard reads it (ADR 0162).
  `blocking` — the drag's stop — read the world boxes of the same pair. A world box *contains* its
  body, so the coarse stop is always at or before the true touch and never after it: the AABB drag
  cannot let a body through, it only stops it early. The slack is a turn's worth: a 200 x 20 board
  turned 45° has a world box reaching x = 141.42 where the board reaches 100.
- The AABB rule is exact in the direction of travel (its world maximum *is* the support function)
  and coarse only in the perpendicular overlap, so the defect is a stop that is too early, not a
  penetration — except for a pair whose world boxes already overlap, where `blocking` answers
  `None` and the drag is unpoliced until the release is refused.
- The second gate was `is_furniture` in `Project::move_rule`: it switched the workspace's snapping
  off for anything that is not a product or inside one, so a loose body could not land on the floor
  plane or the 1 mm grid. Nothing else in the drag was furniture-specific.

## Decisions

- One reading per moved node, not per pair: `Mover { box_, faces }`, where `faces` is
  `!Project::is_furniture(id)`. A furniture piece keeps the world-box contact exactly as it was;
  every other solid body is measured by its own faces. A mixed selection answers each mover with
  its own reading.
- The face reading is a **swept SAT along one world axis**: for each of the 15 axes, the projection
  gap closes at `direction · normal`, so that axis stops separating at `gap / rate`; the touch is
  the largest such distance, an axis that never closes answers `None`, and a pair already sharing
  an axis stays the maker's to repair (the same reading as `resting_inside`). `separating_axes` is
  now shared by `meets` and the drag, so the two cannot disagree about the axis set.
- Rounding at a contact is `quantised` (nearest µm), not floor: the guard's threshold is
  `CONTACT_MM` = 1 µm, so a half-micrometre overshoot is still contact, not a refusal. Flooring cost
  a micrometre to float error (`440.0` came back as `439.999`) and changed a furniture answer.
- Snapping: every selection is handed the settings now. A face-measured mover takes the planes and
  the grid and **not** another part's world box, because a face landing on a box is a gap the solid
  does not have; a furniture mover keeps the frames and the part faces.
- Tool renamed end to end: `TransformMode::Object`, `Transform::Object`, `MoveKind::Object`,
  `TRANSFORM_OBJECT` = `view.transform.object`, `Message::Object`, `ObjectHint`, `ObjectHold`,
  `take_body`. `Project::furniture_axes` became `move_axes`. `RibbonFurniture` and the domain word
  *furniture* stay.

## Tests and their numbers

- A turned board (200 x 20 x 10, 45° about Z) against a 100³ neighbour at x = 500: the world box
  stops it at 358.58, its own corner meets the neighbour's face at exactly **400** — the board's tip
  (500, 141.42) is above the neighbour's y = 100 face and never touches. This is the regression the
  face reading buys.
- A solid body takes the floor plane and the 1 mm grid and is not captured by a neighbour's face
  2 mm away; the same pair still stops at contact at 102.
- Furniture answers are unchanged: the panel axis, the floor class's height, 440 mm for the turned
  cabinet, the frame's face capture.

## Ruled out

- Per-pair exactness (`faces = not furniture on either side`) — a cabinet would then be stopped by
  the tip of a turned lamp, which is a change to the furniture rule the owner did not ask for.
- Keeping the drag's floor-to-micrometre rounding — it costs the last micrometre of a furniture
  contact and does not buy safety, because the guard's threshold is the same micrometre.
- Face capture for a solid body from a neighbour's world box — it would land the piece inside the
  neighbour on a turned face. Left as debt until the snap candidate is the exact contact surface.
- A second rule module or a second mode: the furniture code paths are the object code paths with a
  `Mover` flag, and the free move still asks `contact_rule` (contact, no snaps).
