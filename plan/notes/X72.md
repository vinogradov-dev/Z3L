# X72 — the rule lives in the document, and both moves stop at contact

## What was measured

- The rule existed in one place: `blocking` in `crates/model/src/project/move_query.rs` was read by
  `stop_at_contact` and by nothing else. `Project::set_node_placement` only checked that the node
  exists and is not the root; `node.placement.set`, `set_many` and `panel.create` therefore wrote any
  placement they were handed, and `Project::move_rule` answered `None` for a selection no product
  holds, so the furniture move carried a loose board through everything (the second open end).
- The free move read no rule at all (`MoveRule::limit` was never asked): `constrained_distance(None,
  …)` returned the pointer's distance untouched.
- The frame test `the_free_move_crosses_a_neighbour_the_furniture_move_stops_at` did not actually
  measure a crossing: its 150-point drag travelled less than the 50 mm gap, so `free_end > free_start`
  held with or without a rule. Raising the drag to 400 points is what makes the stop visible.
- A world box is too coarse to *refuse* from. The fill's `Shape` part is the section between two
  leaned posts: its world box and a post's world box overlap deeply while the faces only touch, so
  the first version of the guard (per-axis shared volume, world boxes) refused
  `the_shape_mode_creates_the_whole_section_as_the_ghost_draws_it` and
  `a_beveled_fill_is_one_click_and_one_undo`. A refusal cannot be conservative; a stop can.
- `sample_project`'s shelf and the drawer's front share one box, and `sample_product_project` stands
  its product over both. That fixture overlap is why eight engine `panel.create` tests failed when
  the create path was guarded: the second board was born inside the first.

## Decisions

- The contact test became one function both callers read (`resting_inside`): the write asks
  `Project::resting_conflict`, the drag asks `MoveRule::limit`, so a piece stops where the write would
  refuse.
- "No worse" is read as "no **new** overlap": `was` overlapping means the pair is no refusal, which is
  what lets a pre-rule file be repaired by hand. The alternative — comparing how deep the overlap is
  — needs a depth measure that a rotated pair does not have in a well-defined way, and it turns a
  coincident pair of boards (which `sample_project` holds) into a piece that cannot be dragged at all.
- The refusal is exact: `SurfaceBox::meets` is a separating-axis test over the two boxes' own axes and
  the nine cross products, with `CONTACT_MM` (one micrometre) as the contact slack. The move rule keeps
  the world box: a conservative stop is a millimetre, a wrong refusal is a command.
- `MoveRule::snaps` became `Option<SnapSettings>`: `None` is "no snapping and no grid", which is the
  free tool and the furniture tool on a piece no product holds. One type answers both, so the axes,
  the contact and the snapping have one home.
- `panel.create` is not guarded. A created part has no `was`, so every overlap is new, and its box is
  the rectangle around an outline that may be a trapezoid (the splayed fill) or a beveled ring (the
  leaned wall) — the fill's own flush fit would be refused. The fill's void is measured analytically
  (ADR 0106); the placement the maker then edits is guarded like any other write.

## Ruled out

- **Guarding inside `Project::set_node_placement`.** Model tests and the app fixtures place overlapping
  parts deliberately (`turned_panel` at the origin, the fill's posts), so the guard belongs in the
  command, where `class_rules` already keeps the creation gate (N4).
- **A depth comparison for "worse".** The per-axis shared volume grows while a board is already
  inside another's span and shrinks otherwise, so it is not monotone along the drag, and it froze two
  coincident boards in both directions.
- **A polygon (outline) test instead of a box.** It would fix the splayed trapezoid and not the
  beveled ring, because the bevel is what makes the nominal rectangle reach into the wall's plane.
- **Guarding `panel.contour.create`.** It has no surface (see the debt entry); the same reasoning as
  `panel.create` applies when one takes it.
