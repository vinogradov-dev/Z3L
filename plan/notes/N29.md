# N29 — the niche starts from the frame's footprint

what a session measured about a shaped frame, and why the cell takes an arc by its chord

## What was measured

- `Project::niche` (`crates/model/src/project/niche.rs`) seeded the cell with `Cell::envelope(size)`: the
  six envelope planes, built from the box alone. `SurfaceBox` for an envelope is
  `surface_box(id, ZERO, size, world, Shell)`, so the footprint never reached the cell — a probe crossed
  the corner a cut had removed and stopped at the box, `Niche::cut` answered the box's rectangle, and
  `FillMode::Fit` therefore answered a part outside the case (the N29 entry's Why).
- `Footprint::outline(x, y)` (ADR 0159) already draws the ring the frame reserves — a rectangle with
  corner features, or a stored `Contour` — and validates it, so the niche needed no second shape code.
  The ring is analytic lines and arcs; the fill's whole path (`cut`, `largest_rectangle`, `wall_bevels`,
  `classify_outline`) is polygon-only.
- `pick` in the shell already tested a product by the outline's prism and its area × height, so a
  containment rule by the ring was not a new idea, only an absent one in the model.
- Cost of a ring seed, measured on an empty 720-tall case with nothing inside it (dev profile,
  opt-level 2, 200 measurements per row): 4 sides 15.6 µs, 8 sides 16.3 µs, 16 sides 42.8 µs, 24 sides
  54.6 µs per niche, `holds` and the ring generation included. The corner enumeration is O(planes³), which
  is why the budget below matters and why a rectangle — the overwhelmingly common frame — is unchanged.

## Decisions

- **The cell is seeded from the ring, its edges being planes** (ADR 0201). `Cell::footprint` walks the
  footprint's outline, drops the redundancy a rectangle brings (its four sides are the four box planes
  the cell already had), and keeps the two height planes. Every rule of the cell — `rebuild`,
  `contains`, `cuts`, `clipped`, `holds_body`, `cut_planes` — is untouched, so the candidate planes a
  probe adds still clip the same way and a box case answers bit for bit what it answered before.
- **An arc is taken by its chord.** The alternative — a dense polyline — is what the plane budget
  forbids: a 1 µm chord tolerance on an 80 mm radius is 126 planes and the budget is 32. Chords are also
  the safe direction, since a convex ring's arc bulges away from the shape it cuts, so the chord lies
  inside the frame; a fill therefore stands inside the piece and the rounded case still classifies as
  `Rect` with one corner feature (a chamfer where the frame is round).
- **A ring that turns right is its own refusal.** `seed_polygon` turns the ring counter-clockwise
  (a drawn ring may run either way) and then requires every joint to turn left and every arc to sweep
  left; a notch or an inward arc is `NicheRefusal::Footprint`, mapped to `FillRefusal::Footprint`, to
  `VoidRefusal::Footprint` and to the sentence in `crates/text`. Testing convexity on the *chord*
  polygon alone would accept a ring with an inward arc — that shape's chords are convex while the
  material between the chord and the arc is the frame's — so the joint test is on the ring itself.
- **`Envelope::holds(point)`** is the containment rule, and the niche and the innermost-product search
  both read it: a point in the cut-away corner is `Outside`, not a cell without a floor. The distance
  half of it (`Region::boundary_distance` within one micrometre) exists because a corner stored exactly
  on the cut face is a boundary point, and `contains` alone calls it out.
- **The plane budget went 16 → 32** and the ring is refused past it rather than measured with a wall
  dropped; `MAX_CELL_CORNERS` stayed 64, which a prism of up to 30 sides fills.

## Ruled out

- A cell that holds arcs: `Niche::cut` returns a polygon, `wall_bevels` and `largest_rectangle` are
  polygon algorithms, and the debt entry for a curved *body* already names the work. Sharing it with a
  curved footprint is one change, not two, and it is filed rather than half-done here.
- Sampling the ring for the section, so that `classify_outline` could be given a polygon: a sampled
  arc is many short lines and the classifier matches segment for segment, so a rounded corner would
  have become a contour with a hundred segments instead of a rectangle with one feature.
- Refusing a shaped frame and keeping the box: that is the defect the task reports.
- A per-product cache of the generated ring: the measurement is per 0.5 mm of pointer travel
  (`ui::MEASURE_STEP_MM`) and the whole niche costs tens of microseconds, so a cache would be a key and
  an invalidation rule for no reading a maker can see.

## Verification

- Headless, model: `a_corner_cabinet_reserves_its_cut_corner_and_no_more` (pentagon section, ten corners,
  seven planes, the cut corner is `Outside`), `a_drawn_ring_bounds_the_niche_by_its_own_ring`,
  `a_rounded_corner_is_measured_as_the_chord_its_radius_spans`, `a_footprint_that_turns_right_is_a_refusal_of_its_own`;
  `a_cut_corner_case_fits_the_largest_rectangle_inside_its_cut` (500 × 500, not the box's 600 × 400),
  `a_cut_corner_case_takes_its_own_outline_in_the_shape_mode` (one chamfer of 100/100),
  `the_part_a_shaped_case_creates_stands_inside_its_footprint` (all three fills, both modes, the created
  panel's own ring sampled and tested in the product frame), `a_rounded_corner_stands_as_the_chord_inside_its_arc`,
  `a_frame_whose_ring_turns_right_is_refused_and_builds_no_part`; `Envelope::holds` has four tests
  including the point between a radius' chord and its arc.
- Headless, UI: `a_frame_whose_ring_turns_right_says_so_of_itself` proves the atom carries the new
  refusal to the one sentence every surface prints.
- Not verified by a live run: nothing under the shell draws differently, because the ghost and the tool
  card read the plan and the atom, and the shell's own chip lists were already generic. The niche tool's
  overlay still draws the cell's **box**, which is a summary of the cell and not the cell; that and the
  chord an arc becomes are the two entries this task filed in `plan/debt.md`.
