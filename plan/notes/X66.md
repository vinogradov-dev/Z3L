# X66 — the carried piece's distance card takes the hand

## What was measured

- `tool_card::show` draws an `egui::Area` on `Order::Middle` at `pointer + CARD_GAP` (18 pt), so the
  card is normally *beside* the hand and its plates are out of reach.
- `constrain_to(viewport)` breaks that: the card cannot hang past the edge, so near the right and the
  bottom of the work area it is pulled back — and with the side panels folded there is nothing in
  between, so the card lands *over* the pointer (measured: card `[1041 739]-[1272 799]` holding the
  pointer at `1156 769`).
- On the furniture move's hold the pointer owns nothing, so a press over a row is a press on a plate:
  the row's `Scrub` takes the drag (its unfocused branch scrubs and commits live), the number pins the
  gap, and the piece jumps to it and stops following the hand. The click that was meant to put the
  piece down never reaches the viewport.
- The plate is *also* the only keyboard path: `DistanceFields::show` asks for the keyboard and the
  nearest row takes it, which is how a gap is typed. So the card cannot simply be disabled.

## Decisions

- `tool_card::show` gains `sense_pointer` and passes it to `Area::interactable`: a card without the
  pointer is click-through (egui skips a non-interactable layer in the hit test) and keeps the
  keyboard, so typing a gap still works and the press falls through to the viewport.
- `DistanceFields::show` forwards the flag unchanged; every other gesture passes `true`, because a
  fill, a pull or a box holds a button and aims at the plate on purpose.
- The document decides: `sense_pointer = !translate_gizmo.is_following()`.

## Ruled out

- Hiding the plates while a piece is carried: the live gaps are the reason a maker carries a shelf.
- Moving the plates away from the hand: they are placed at the gap they measure, which is where the
  hand already is.
- Dropping the piece on the *press* instead of the click: it would fix the symptom here and change the
  gesture the owner approved (a click puts it down).
