# X83 — the leap was read as travel, so no frame showed the face

the leap was read as travel, so no frame showed the face

## What the three logs show

- Single frames carry 500-1000 mm of pointer travel at this zoom (`asked=(987.6,-483)`, `(1157,-447)`).
  A piece 520 long that must pass a body 40 thick needs 560 mm: one frame.
- The hold compared the answer's step with the hand's own step (`step > hand_step + 15`), and with a
  quick hand the two are equal — so a leap across a whole body was read as ordinary travel and no
  frame ever showed the piece resting on the face. That is the "прыгнул тут же".
- The other direction read differently only because the leftover margin was on another side of the
  comparison; the log has `jump=None` in every frame of the newest run, i.e. the hold never fired.
- The Y door is honest: clearing 340 mm of the neighbour's depth is a legitimate place. It *looks*
  like passing through because the top view draws the piece inside the neighbour's outline.

## Fixed here

- `MoveRule::crossing(from, to)` — the first body a straight carry passes through, on the same swept
  face test the axis drag uses (`blocking_along`, now reading a unit direction, so a path of any length
  answers millimetres). A granted place beyond it is held `JUMP_HOLD` whatever the hand did.
- `MoveRule::enclosing(point)` — the body the hand's own point stands inside; while it names one the
  hold keeps age 0, so the wait is the cursor crossing the body's thickness. A shell never gates.
- The hold fires only on a place the rule grants whole: a cut answer is the face, already seen.
- `blocking_along` normalisation bug found on the way: the old axis-only reading was millimetres by
  accident (the direction was a unit axis), a general path divided gaps by the path's length.

## Ruled out

- The destination rule (0166) is right: the free place must be free for the whole piece.
- Contact by faces is right: `meets` is swept SAT; nothing was needed on the surface side.
