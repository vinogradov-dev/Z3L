# X65 — a carried piece runs away from the cursor

## What was measured

- Reproduced in `divider_beside_a_panel` (a quarter-turned divider beside a panel, 50 mm apart, the
  product open): a hand that moved up and left carried the board from a 50 mm contact to −715 mm in
  four frames of `hover`, in the *opposite* screen direction to its travel. The piece was still
  following the pointer in the sense that every frame recomputed it — the number was just wrong.
- The cause was the distance mapping borrowed from the handle drag: `ray_to(pointer).closest_parameter_on_line(origin,
  axis)` and a difference of parameters. A handle is grabbed within a few points of the line, where
  the projection is well conditioned; a carried piece is grabbed by its *body*, up to hundreds of
  points off the line, and the closest point on a line to a ray that does not pass near it runs along
  the line as the pointer strays sideways.
- Numbers from `gizmo_math::tests::the_travel_is_the_hand_s_own_and_the_ray_s_is_unstable`: with the
  default view, a *purely sideways* move of the hand by 200 points moves the ray's projection onto the
  Z axis by 69.7 mm. The divider fixture, whose axis is oblique, turned that into hundreds.
- The divider probe: the contact at +50 mm held while the hand moved along X, and the piece jumped to
  −51 mm the moment the hand moved 30 points *up* — the sign flip the report saw as "runs away".
- The fields, the capture and the rule all read `drag_delta` and the latched axis, so they need no
  change: the defect is entirely in how the scalar is derived from the pointer.
- `axis_handles`/`named_axis` already project the axis' unit screen direction at the pivot and skip the
  axes within 10° of the view direction, so the screen frame was one function away.

## Decisions

- The distance is the hand's *screen* travel along the axis' projected direction, multiplied by the
  millimetres one point of that travel means at the anchor. The slope is measured the way the old
  code measured, one point along the screen direction, which is exact at the anchor and linear after
  it: bounded, monotone and identical for every pointer that travels the same way.
- `travel_frame(camera, viewport, origin, axis, pointer)` answers the unit direction and the slope
  together, and refuses an axis that points at the camera (the same 10° rule the handles use).
- `Travel { from, covered, direction, per_point }` is the anchor: `distance(pointer)` is
  `covered + (pointer - from)·direction · per_point`. `covered` is what keeps the piece still across
  an anchor, and an anchor is set when the axis is named and on every frame that drives the camera —
  the same two moments N25 already needed.
- The held handle keeps the ray's projection: a handle *is* the line, its dead zone keeps the pointer
  near it, and its distance is the one the maker aimed at.
- `named_axis` now returns the projected `AxisHandle` rather than the bare axis, so the direction and
  the axis come from one pass and cannot disagree.

## Ruled out

- A camera-plane free delta clamped per axis: it needs a plane the tool does not have (a carry has one
  axis), and it re-scales with the pointer's depth, so a piece would speed up as it came closer.
- Keeping the ray's projection and widening the dead zone: the instability is geometric, not a
  threshold; the piece would still be wrong at a distance.
- Re-latching the axis or re-anchoring every frame: the first restarts the distance on every flip, the
  second cancels itself out (an anchor that is recomputed from the current delta keeps the delta).
- Clamping the distance to the piece's own size: a carry legitimately crosses a 3000 mm case.
