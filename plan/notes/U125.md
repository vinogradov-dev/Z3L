# U125 — a row of boards spread evenly from the first one

## What was measured

- The plan is one part: `FillPlan` (`crates/model/src/project/fill.rs`) carries `outline`,
  `nominal_size`, `placement`, `niche`, `corners`, `far_corners`, `trims`, `bevels` and the two ends
  `free_low`/`free_high`; `Project::fill_plan_in` is the one plan behind `fill_plan`, and it reads the
  free-axis place out of `FillRequest` — `free: Option<f64>`, `None` meaning the point under the
  pointer chooses it.
- `FillMode` has two variants, `Fit` and `Shape`, and it is a **wire word** of `panel.fill`: a mode
  says how one part reads the section, and one command makes one part.
- The free axis of a fill is `free_axis(fill)`: a shelf grows upward, a post across the void, a wall
  in depth — the panel plane normal the owner names is this axis.
- The gesture holds one ghost: `FillGizmo` (`app/src/fill_gizmo.rs`) keeps a `FillGhost` planned by
  `plan_at`, and `Document::update_fill` re-plans it per frame from the niche atom;
  `Document::fill_place` turns the pointer into the free place in whole millimetres.
- The click writes one part: `Document::create_fill` dispatches one `panel.create` through
  `Editor::create_panel_sized`.
- The plate at the hand is the two gap fields of `Distances`; its `rows` is the one place a gap
  becomes a row, and `Document::fill_rows` adds the class, build and mounting choice rows on top.
- A batch is one operation: `Session::batch` runs the steps on a copy and installs it as one undo
  entry with one revision (X128, ADR 0234), so a row of N `panel.create` steps is one undo entry —
  the commit path existed and needed no new command.

## What the owner decided (2026-11-25)

- The evenly fill is the operation itself, not a parameter: like an array, it does the routine and
  builds several boards instead of one. It is chosen on the tool card beside the build («Одна доска» /
  «Равномерно»); nothing stores it — not the document, not the workspace settings.
- The gesture listens to the hand first: for a shelf the pointer up or down grows or shrinks the
  count, for a post it is left or right, for a wall it is along the depth — in every case the fill's
  `free_axis`, the panel plane normal.
- **Three fields**: the two distances to the walls (the same two the single fill draws) and the
  count. Typing a distance fixes it. Typing a count draws the phantoms at once and is clamped to the
  boards that fit without crossing; it is a clamp, not a refusal.
- The layout (the picture `Paneloj-Prost-6.png`): with the near distance fixed — by the click or by a
  typed number — the boards are spread evenly and the leftover falls at the far end. In numbers:
  `g = (S − L − N·t)/N`, where the interior gaps and the trailing gap are that one `g`. A typed far
  gap `H` fixes it instead and the neighbours divide the rest: `g = (S − L − H − N·t)/(N − 1)`.

## Decisions

- **The model query is `model::fill_row(niche, thickness, axis, ask)`**, with `FillPlan::row` as the
  convenience: the arithmetic reads only the void's span along the axis and the board's thickness, so
  the shell asks it with the numbers a `FillGhost` already holds — no second `FillPlan` cloned into
  the row state. The count is `floor(span/t)` clamped to the boards that fit; a typed count claims the
  whole void; a typed far gap is honoured and the neighbours share the rest.
- **The commit is a batch of `panel.create`, one per board** (`Session::fill_row`, `FillRowCreate`):
  one revision, one undo entry, one report (J4, X128). Undo takes the whole row back; a refusal lands
  nothing, because the batch is all-or-nothing. The boards are named after the word with the numbers
  no sibling carries (X63) and placed one pitch apart along the axis.
- **The anchor is a ghost, not a write**: the first click freezes the board the row repeats, the
  second click writes. `RowState { anchor, direction, plan, count }` lives in `FillGizmo`, so Escape
  drops it with no node and no revision, and the tool stays armed.
- **The count is a `ToolParam::Count` row drawn by the shared card**: `DistanceFields::show` gained
  one optional extra row and `DistanceOutcome` carries the number; the field policy (scrub, Enter,
  Escape, the focus) is the shared one, so the row costs no second plate (ADR 0092, ADR 0119).
- **A row reads its two pins apart** (`Distances::pinned_start`, `pinned_gap`) instead of
  `pinned_low`: a row is not one rigid part, and the near face and the far gap are two different
  numbers of one gesture.
- **A focused field that measures no direction still owns Escape**: it gives the keyboard back and the
  tool's own Escape is the next press (`DistanceFields::owns_the_keyboard`, ADR 0103).
- **The status bar names the count** (`StatusInfo::row`, `Message::StatusRow`), read from the model's
  plan like the ghost is, so the bar and the card cannot disagree.

## Ruled out

- **`FillMode::Even`**: the mode says how one part reads the section and travels on the wire as a
  parameter of a command that creates exactly one panel; a row of N panels is not a property of one
  part, so the choice sits beside the mode in the gesture.
- **A `panel.fill_row` command**: the registry would need a command that creates N nodes, and
  `Session::batch` already makes N commands one entry, one revision and one report (J4).
- **A second plate for the count**: the shared card draws any `ToolRow`, so the count is one more row
  of the same card and the same scrub.
- **Rounding the gaps to whole millimetres**: only the hand's place is whole millimetres (it already
  was); the gaps are the exact share of the void, so a row of ten does not drift off the wall.
- **A rigid `Distances` part for the row**: its pins hold a part's extent, and a row's extent is what
  the gaps decide — hence the two accessors above instead of `pinned_low`.
