# X92 — the probe reads a body the boxes cannot describe, and no other

## What was measured

- The port's `ray` and the lazy shape build landed in X90; the probe still answered from
  `SurfaceBox::face_hit` alone, and `probe`/`niche` took the adapter as `_contact`. Measured against an
  ordinary scene, handing that adapter to every surface moved a panel's plane by rounding and broke
  `the_fill_ghost_passes_the_board_it_meets_and_holds_at_the_frame_envelope` (X90).
- So the question is not "does the probe ask the port" but **which** surface it asks about. A panel is
  its box, to the micrometre; what the box cannot describe is a body whose own surface leaves a hole in
  itself (a plate with an opening) or never reaches the corners of its box (a ball, a cone). Those are
  the only bodies measured wrongly by a box, and there are two of them, not ten.

## What landed

1. `contact_shape::shape_beyond_box(geometry, local_min, local_max) -> Option<Shape>` — the body's own
   surface only when the box does not describe it, `None` when the box is the honest reading. The
   decision is `describes_box`: a hull whose points are the box's eight corners, or a triangle skin
   whose whole area equals the box's own and whose triangles all lie in its faces. A shape that reaches
   outside the box the caller keeps answers `None` too, because the probe asks the box first.
2. `niche::Surface` — a surface box plus the geometry its body is built from and a `OnceLock` for the
   prepared body. A panel, an envelope and a shell carry no geometry at all; only an object may. The
   body is built the first time one question's ray reaches for it and is shared by every other ray of
   that question, so a scene of a few hundred bodies builds the handful a probe could meet — the same
   laziness the moving rule got in X90. `body_surfaces` now hands the geometry it already has to the
   surface instead of being walked a second time.
3. `Surface::face_hit` asks the box first and the port last: a ray whose box it misses cannot meet the
   body, and a body the ray misses — down a hole, past the corner of a ball — answers no face at all
   where the box would have answered a wall. The `SurfaceBox` broad phase in `first_face` is unchanged.
4. Three tests: `a_probe_crosses_the_hole_and_stops_at_the_floor_behind_it` (the scene fixture, a case
   with the ring plate across it: 145 mm by the box, 450 mm to the case's floor by the port, and the
   board beside the hole measured identically both ways), `an_ordinary_cell_keeps_the_planes_the_boxes_gave_it`
   (one niche with and without the adapter, equal field by field, with a baked box standing as a wall)
   and `a_probe_meets_a_balls_own_side_and_not_the_corner_of_its_box` (78.6 mm against the box's 50).
   Two unit tests pin the decision itself, including a skin one triangle short of a closed box.

## Ruled out

- **Asking the port for every surface and preferring the box answer when the two agree per ray.** Same
  shape-building cost as the split, a comparison per ray, and the decision would be taken per ray where
  the plan asks for once per surface; the classification is where the knowledge is.
- **Keeping the port out of the probe until a boolean can author a hole.** The plate with a hole is
  already a scene this tree writes and measures (X89), and a hidden body inside a cell is a hole the
  probe must cross to see the wall behind it.
- **Deciding exactness from the geometry kind alone** (`Primitive` → port, `Mesh` → box). An L-shaped
  extrusion is a mesh whose box is wrong, and a cuboid object is a mesh whose box is exactly right.
