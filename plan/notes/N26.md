# N26 — a carried piece's gaps are dimensioned in the scene

## What was measured

- `DistanceFields::show` in `crates/ui/src/distance.rs` did four things in one body: Tab and the
  fresh-gesture focus, the keyboard request, `tool_card::show` at an anchor, and Escape/Enter after
  the draw. Both layouts need the first, the request and the last, so they became `begin`/`end` and
  `show` kept only the card.
- The carry's card was drawn with `interactable(false)` (X66), which meant its fields already took
  the keyboard and never the pointer. A per-gap plate is the same rule at a smaller size: the whole
  apparatus of a card that "keeps the keyboard and never the pointer" was there to serve one plate.
- `tool_card::show` returned the card's rectangle for `Chrome::tool`, and `Document::tool_card_rect`
  is what `main.rs` hands to it. A carried piece needs no rectangle at all: with no card there is no
  chrome over the scene, so the plates are click-through by geometry and no chrome list had to grow.
- `gizmo_math::travel_frame` already answers `None` for an axis within 10° of the view direction
  (`VIEW_ALIGNMENT_LIMIT = 0.985`), and its unit test `the_travel_is_the_hand_s_own_and_the_ray_s_is_unstable`
  asserts it for a top view. Using it as the arrow's gate keeps one predicate for "this axis has no
  screen direction".
- `Project::probe` is a ray from one point along one direction, measured against each surface's own
  box (`SurfaceBox::face_hit`), not against a world box. A fixture whose neighbour is a flat board
  covering only one Z band therefore finds no wall at the other board's face centre: the frame test
  needs a piece with the surface in front of both of its faces, not merely a neighbour whose world
  box overlaps.
- `continue_follow` put the pinned distance through `constrained_distance` (the rule and its snaps)
  while `continue_handle` did not; the same typed number could therefore be snapped away in one tool
  and honoured in the other.
- The inspector had no move rows at all: `tool_view` answered the fill arm's `Distances` and nothing
  else, so the card was the only surface for a drag's two gaps.

## Decisions

- `DistanceFields::show_in_scene` draws one `tool_card::plate` per placement and returns
  `DistanceOutcome::plates: [Option<Rect>; 2]`; `rect` stays `None`, which is how the shell says "no
  chrome over the scene".
- The arrow is painted by the shell (`paint_gap_arrow`) from the same two world points the field's
  position is read from, in `theme::helper`, dimmed by `gamma_multiply` while the pointer is away
  from the run; the heads are the drafting grammar's terminators and a run shorter than two heads
  draws the line alone.
- The arrowhead tip is the witness and the plate covers the middle of the run, so the plate hides
  part of its own dimension line: the number is what the maker sets, and the heads still show which
  two surfaces the number is between.
- `tool_view` answers the move's rows when no tool card owns the inspector, and `apply_tool_outcome`
  routes a gap edit to the live measurement (`self.distances`, else `move_distances`), so the
  inspector is one more surface of the same `Distances` rather than a second draft.
- `continue_follow` takes a typed distance exactly as `continue_handle` does: the maker's number is
  not the pointer's, and no rule may move it (ADR 0103's own sentence, which the carry broke).
- `sense_pointer` left `tool_card::show` with its last `false` caller: a plate is the one surface that
  holds the keyboard without the pointer, and it is always that.

## Ruled out

- Keeping the card and drawing the arrows beside it: two surfaces for one number, and the card is
  exactly what covers the pointer and drifts after a camera gesture (X66, X69).
- One `Area` per gap with `interactable(true)`: a carried piece holds no button, so the plate would
  take the press that puts the piece down and scrub the number the piece would then jump to.
- A single union rectangle in `Chrome::tool` for the two plates: a union of two plates on opposite
  sides of the piece covers the piece and the scene between them, so the drop click would have to
  miss the middle of the object it is dropping.
- Drawing the gap only where the pointer is: the piece is the thing being measured, and a maker
  types one gap while looking at the other.
- A separate dimension surface in `crates/ui`: the shell owns the projection and the paint, and the
  field anatomy is already `tool_card`'s row.
- Re-anchoring the card to the piece instead of to the hand: X69's answer is the carry's own hand,
  and the number still would not be where the gap is.
