# U109 — the floor is blended, and its shadow rides the same alpha

## What was measured

U108 had already made the plate write no depth, so a `100 %` floor let a part standing at `-500 mm`
draw *over* it, untinted. The plate was drawn first in the view pass, and that place is what had to
move: a blended plate can only tint a body it is drawn after.

The one number the design turns on is the composite. With the plate's opacity `a`, the shadow's
strength `s` and the mask's coverage `c`, the old fragment wrote `mix(plate, shadow, c·s)` at alpha
1. Keeping that mix and taking the alpha as `max(a, c·s)` means:

- `a = 1`: alpha 1 and the old mix — the 100 % picture is the old picture, bit for bit;
- `a = 0.4`, no shadow: `0.4·plate + 0.6·behind`, the tint the owner asked for;
- `a = 0.4`, `c·s = 0.9`: alpha `0.9`, so the shadow keeps its weight instead of being blended away
  with the rest of the plate — a transparent floor cannot erase its own shadow;
- `a = 0`: an explicit zero, because `max()` alone would still paint a shadow over an invisible floor,
  and a shadow needs a surface to darken.

## Decisions

- **The plate is drawn after the opaque faces, not after the translucent ones.** The ghost and the
  wireframe styles write no depth at all, so an opaque plate drawn after a blended face paints the
  floor over the model: the first attempt put the plate after the translucent pass and the
  wireframe shot showed the plate covering the model. The opaque pass is the only depth writer, so
  the plate goes immediately after it; the grid follows it on the same surface.
- **A CPU-side pure rule where there is one.** The opacity is clamped in `sanitize_opacity` and
  written into the uniform with `camera_uniform`, which became a free function so a test can assert
  the slot without a device. The `max()` rule itself lives in the fragment and is asserted by a
  picture, not by a mirror of the shader in Rust.
- **One uniform slot, not a buffer key.** `shadow_plane` was full, so the opacity took the next
  `vec4` of the same uniform: a drag writes 16 bytes and re-uploads nothing, which the shell test
  asserts through `Display::repaints` staying false.

## Ruled out

- **Keeping the plate first and blending it there.** The plate would blend against the background
  and every body would be drawn over it untinted — the U108 picture.
- **Writing depth when the floor is opaque.** Two behaviours for one surface, and at 100 % it would
  bring back the culled part below the floor that U108 removed.
- **A second blended layer for the shadow.** It needs a second pass, a second attachment or a read
  of what is behind the plate, which a fragment cannot do.
- **A CPU branch that skips the plate at 0 %.** The rule belongs to the value, not to the pass loop;
  the shader's `select` covers the case in one place, and the draw call stays unconditional.

## What the tests say

- `a_see_through_floor_tints_the_part_below_it_and_keeps_its_shadow` (GPU): one scene at 100 %,
  40 % and 0 %. The part below the floor is the plate's own colour at 100 %, plainly its own colour
  at 0 %, and strictly between the two at 40 %; the shadowed patch of floor is at least 15 levels
  darker than the lit one at both 40 % and 100 %, and at 40 % it stays below 40 % of the lit patch —
  which the same test fails at 53 % when the `max()` rule is removed; at 0 % the floor patches clear
  to the frame's background.
- `the_floor_plate_blends_so_it_can_tint_what_stands_under_it` asserts the plate's target state, so
  the pipeline cannot go back to opaque without a red gate; the depth state keeps `U108`'s
  no-write.
- `the_floor_opacity_reaches_the_uniform_clamped_and_never_costs_a_buffer` covers 1, 0.4, 0, a
  negative and a NaN, and reads the frame's own value through the same slot.
- The settings tests round-trip 35 %, clamp a hand-edited 900 to 100, and load a file written before
  the field as the opaque floor.

## What the pictures say

`target/ui-shots/floor-opacity-100.png` against `floor-opacity-40.png` (a shelf moved to
`(150, -500, -500)`): at 100 % the shelf is behind an opaque plate, at 40 % it is seen through the
tint with the grid drawn over it and the panel's shadow still black. One thing worth knowing: at
100 % the shelf's *form outlines* still show, because the plate writes no depth (U108) — an
underground object reads as a wireframe ghost rather than a solid one, which the ADR records.
