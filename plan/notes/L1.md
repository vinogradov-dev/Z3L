# L1 — the shadow map is the key light's depth pass, and the ground's mask taught it

## What was measured

The ground mask of ADR 0142 (`crates/render/src/ground.rs`) is a *coverage* render into one plane: the
scene projected along the key light onto `z = 0`, 1024², blurred to a 60 mm penumbra. It answers one
question about one receiver, so `fs_mesh` (`crates/render/src/scene.wgsl`) read no texture at all and
two panels meeting at a corner were lit as if nothing stood between them.

The renderer had four groups of GPU state and two frames' worth of keying already: `GroundKey` on
`generation`, the quantised `key_direction` and the grid's half-size; the upload cache keyed by mesh,
transform and colour (X57); the world-space vertex buffers of every drawable, which is what a depth
pass needs and why it costs no geometry; and `Frustum`/`Aabb` for the scene's box (X56, X58).

The demo picture that made the task worth doing: `target/ui-shots/shadow-map-shelf.png` against
`shadow-map-open.png` — a shelf 300 mm in front of a panel darkens the 190 mm band under it, and the
same cabinet with the shelf out is uniformly lit.

## Decisions

- **A depth map, not a second coverage mask.** A panel is any plane at any angle, so the question is
  "the nearest surface along this ray", which a coverage render into one plane cannot answer.
  2048², `Depth32Float`, over the sphere around the scene's box: the sphere keeps the map square in
  the light's own axes, so a turned sun changes the picture and not the resolution.
- **The bias is the design.** A receiver is sampled at a point moved along its own normal by 1.5 map
  texels, scaled by `1 + tan θ` up to five, plus a depth bias of `(1.5 + 2.5 tan θ) / 2048`. The
  comparison is `LessEqual` so the two coplanar cases — a band on a face, a shelf flush with a side —
  are *lit* rather than seamed; each has a pixel test.
- **One light, one strength.** `ShadowKey` and `GroundKey` quantise through `crate::quantized_light`,
  so the floor and the panels cannot disagree about the sun. `ContactShadows` keeps its one strength
  and its per-view switch: a face loses `strength` of its key diffuse and specular, leaving ambient
  and the point lights, which is why a shadow reads as a volume rather than as paint.
- **A second bind group, `group(1)`.** `texture_depth_2d` + `sampler_comparison` + the light's
  uniform; the ground's mask moved to `group(2)` because one shader module cannot declare two
  resources at the same binding. The depth pass binds the same group at its own index with a
  uniform-only layout, so a pass never samples the attachment it writes.
- **Comparison sampler with `FilterMode::Nearest` and a 3×3 kernel.** Linear comparison filtering is
  not a capability every backend promises for `Depth32Float`; nine nearest taps are predictable
  everywhere and span two texels, which is about three millimetres — the sun's hard silhouette.

## Ruled out

- **Sampling the existing coverage mask in the face shader.** It holds a silhouette on one plane, not
  a depth, so it would darken a panel only where a body stands *over the floor*, not where one stands
  in front of the panel.
- **A wider PCF kernel for a penumbra.** A penumbra at panel scale needs tens of taps per fragment;
  the ground keeps its separable blur because its receiver is one plane, and a face shadow of two
  texels reads as the line it is.
- **Cascades, a second sun, screen-space AO.** One cabinet is two metres, so one map is finer than a
  cascade at every level; a second sun is not a document entity (ADR 0036); AO cannot darken a
  surface that nothing covers, which is the niche itself.
- **Texel snapping of the light matrix.** It is rebuilt on the same keys as the ground mask, so it is
  never resampled between frames, and snapping would only quantise a rebuild that already happened.

## What the tests say

- `a_shelf_darkens_the_panel_behind_it_and_the_open_cabinet_does_not`: the band under the shelf falls
  by more than 20 levels against the same frame with the shadow off and against the open cabinet; the
  panel above the shelf keeps its light within 3 levels, so the shadow is local.
- `a_box_never_shadows_itself_and_a_flush_band_casts_nothing`: a lone lit box differs from the same box
  with the shadow off by at most 3 levels on any channel, and a band lying at the face's own depth
  changes no pixel — the acne and the coplanar band, in one picture.
- `a_shelf_flush_with_a_side_leaves_the_panel_above_it_lit`: the inner face just above a flush shelf
  is as lit as it is with the shelf taken away.
- `a_shadow_map_is_drawn_on_an_upload_and_a_turned_sun_and_never_on_an_orbit`: the rebuild counter
  says 1, then 1 after two orbits, then 2 on a turned sun, then 3 on an upload.
- One trap found on the way, worth remembering: `Camera::looked_from` only checks orthogonality, not
  handedness. A mirrored basis flips screen-space winding, so every visible face reads as a back face,
  the shader flips its normal inwards and a box shadows itself. The test cameras pass
  `(-X, Z, -Y)` for a view down `-Y`, never `(X, Z, -Y)`.
